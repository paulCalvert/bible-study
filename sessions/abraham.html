Abraham: Growing in Trust — A Lifetime of Walking with God
How does a man learn to walk with God? Abraham is our model — not because he was great, but because he kept walking. Three sections trace his story from the call in Ur through Isaac's birth: learning who God is (the call, Haran, Egypt), two steps forward/one step back (Lot, the war, the covenant hinge of Genesis 15:6, Hagar), and walking steadier but not perfect (circumcision, Abimelech, Isaac) — closing on the sacrifice of Isaac. This was never a story about how great Abraham was. It's a story about how great God is.

Section 1 — Learning More About God

~13 min
Abraham is not remarkable — that's what makes the story remarkable. He's just a guy trying to figure out how to be friends with God, without a Bible, without a godly family, without brothers in the faith. Just a guy and God.
Setting the Scene & the Call (Genesis 11, 12:1–2)
  • We first meet him as Abram, in Ur — pre-Babylon, likely from money and privilege. Close-knit family: his father Terah, wife Sarai, nephew Lot. He had everything except children — and back then, children were your legacy, your security, your future.
  • God shows up and speaks directly into Abram's deepest fear, promising him children — but also calling him to leave his home, his people, his family. In that culture, your identity and security flowed entirely from your clan. God was calling Abram to trust Him alone instead.
Haran: Half-Obedience (Genesis 11:31, 12:1)
  • Abram half-obeys — leaves Ur but brings his father and nephew along, and gets stuck in Haran for decades. Only after Terah dies does Abram finally follow God fully.
  • Learns: God is patient and wants us to come back to Him. How many of us have said some version of "once my career takes off, then I'll make time for God"?
Egypt (Genesis 12:10–20)
  • Famine hits. Instead of asking God for help, Abram looks to Egypt — and, afraid the Egyptians will kill him for his beautiful wife, has Sarai claim to be his sister. He thinks he has it under control. He doesn't: Sarai is immediately taken into Pharaoh's palace.
  • God is faithful anyway — disease strikes Pharaoh's household, Sarai is returned, and Abram is paid off and sent out. He returns to Bethel, back to where he last had a real relationship with God, and worships there.
  • Learns: God doesn't fail His promises even when Abram does.
Table Discussion — Round 1
What have you said you need to take care of before you make time for God?
— or — Have you walked into a bad decision thinking you had it under control? How did that go?

Section 2 — Two Steps Forward, One Step Back

~13 min
Abram is back in Canaan, following God and trusting Him — but the walk doesn't get easier just because it's going well.
Lot, the War, and the King of Sodom (Genesis 13–14)
  • A new problem — leftover wealth from Egypt. Lot picks the lush Jordan Valley and camps near Sodom, thinking about himself rather than God. Abram, after Egypt, has made a decision: even in the desert, God will be there. Lot's wealth owned him; Abram owned his wealth.
  • War breaks out, Lot is carried off, Abram rides out and wins it all back. The King of Sodom offers him the loot — Abram turns it down flat. What Pharaoh had done still irked him; he wasn't going to let a king take credit for what God had done.
  • Growth: Abram is trusting God to provide instead of the world.
The Hinge (Genesis 15)
  • God speaks to Abram after the battle, but Abram has one thing on his mind: "Lord God, what will you give me, for I continue childless?" (15:2). God tells him to look up and count the stars — so shall his offspring be.
  • Then, possibly the most important verse in the entire Bible: "Abram believed God, and it was credited to him as righteousness" (15:6). This is the hinge of salvation — quoted by Paul in Romans and Galatians, by James — the foundation of how God saves anyone: not performance, not achievement, just belief.
  • Learns: God can be trusted.
Hagar — Even After the Hinge (Genesis 16)
  • You'd think he's got it now. He doesn't. Sarai, despairing after decades of waiting, recommends Abram have children through her servant Hagar — culturally normal at the time. Hagar gets pregnant, rivalry erupts, and Hagar is driven out while still pregnant — a messy, completely unnecessary situation.
  • God steps in and redeems it anyway.
  • Learns: God doesn't need us to solve the problems He said He will solve.
Table Discussion — Round 2
Two steps forward, one step back. Where do you see that pattern in your own walk with God?

Section 3 — Walking Steadier, But Not Perfect

~13 min
Circumcision and the New Name (Genesis 17)
  • Twenty-nine years since the promise. God gives Abraham a new sign of the covenant — circumcision — and Abraham does it that very day. No delay, no negotiation. Compare that to Haran: the man who spent decades at a rest stop now moves immediately when God speaks.
  • God renames him Abraham — "father of multitudes" — at 99 years old, with no son by Sarah yet.
  • Growth: Abram obeys God immediately and fully.
Abimelech (Genesis 20)
  • Abraham is on an upward trajectory — and fumbles the exact same mistake as Egypt, passing Sarah off as his sister again. God protects Sarah again, appearing to Abimelech in a dream. Same lesson, twice. God was patient with Abraham. He is patient with us.
  • Learns: I can fail in the same ways again and again, and God will still take me back.
Isaac (Genesis 21:1–7)
  • And then — God kept every word He had spoken. Three phrases in two verses: "as he had said," "what he had promised," "at the very time God had promised." Human doubt, human schemes, human failure — none of it moved the promise or delayed the delivery.
  • He failed early. He failed late. He failed after his biggest growth moments. But he kept walking. And God kept every word.
  • Learns: God keeps His promises.
Table Discussion — Round 3
What are some things you think you should be better at or know better by now?
Optional follow-up: Has God been patient with you in that area — shown up anyway even when you kept stumbling?

Close — The Sacrifice of Isaac

Genesis 22
  • After everything — decades of waiting, failures, growth — Isaac is finally in his arms, the promise fulfilled. And God asks for it all back: "Take your son, your only son Isaac, whom you love, and offer him" (22:2).
  • Not blind faith — sixty to seventy years of evidence. Abraham would have failed this earlier in his walk.
  • The whole morning had been about one thing: how does a man grow to trust God? Abraham's final exam was simple — "Do you trust me with all of it?" He said yes, because everything had taught him God could be trusted with everything.
  • God stopped him. Delivered a ram. Kept His word one more time.
This was never a story about how great Abraham was. It's a story about how great God is.

"Although we cannot be lawgivers, kings, generals, or prophets, we can be what Abram was — a man who heard God and believed that God can be trusted to do what he says he will do, and based his entire life on that conviction." — Boice
