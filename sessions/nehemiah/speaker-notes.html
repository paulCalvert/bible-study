Nehemiah: Innocent as a Dove, Wise as a Serpent

CMBC Men's Breakfast  |  August 2026
Core Thread: How does a man stay innocent as a dove AND wise as a serpent? Nehemiah is our model.
Intro  (~5 min, before prayer)
  • Opener — Hawaii / Bryan Haggling (~1 min): Hawaii recap, then the Bryan haggling story Sets up the 48 Laws bit that follows.
  • The Verse (~30 sec): "Behold, I send you out as sheep in the midst of wolves; so be wise as serpents and innocent as doves." (Matthew 10:16)
  • 48 Laws of Power (~1 min): Greene's manual for the world's power game — use people, never trust, manipulate, press every advantage
    • Serpent wisdom gone wrong
    • "The first will be first."
    • Calls us to be like wolves
  • Kingdom Runs the Other Way (~1 min): Jesus flips it — God's Kingdom is dove rules: innocent, trusting, submitted to Him
    • "The first will be last" (Matthew 19:30)
    • Called to be like sheep
  • Why "Wise as Serpents" Too? (~1 min): Not to run those plays ourselves — wise TO them. See it coming, don't adopt the tactics. Sheep among wolves, not becoming one
  • Preview (~30 sec): Nehemiah kept his innocence, but was wily enough to avoid danger among wolves. Pray → Segment 1
Segment 1 — Two Kings of Two Opposing Kingdoms  (~15 min | 1 Kings 21–22)
  • Setup (~2 min): One kingdom of 12 tribes under David and Solomon, then the split after Solomon's death — Judah (south) mixed but sometimes turned back to God; Israel (north) never had a good king. Israel was later taken captive by Assyria; Judah by Babylon
  • Jehoshaphat, the Dove (~1 min): Good king of Judah. Innocent: yes. Wise: not even close. More on Jehoshaphat apart from Ahab: (1 Kings 22:41-50)
    • Sent officials/Levites to teach the Law in Judah's cities (2 Chronicles 17:7-9)
    • Appointed judges, charged to fear the Lord, not man (2 Chronicles 19:5-7)
  • Ahab, the Serpent (~2 min): Worst king of Israel. More on Ahab apart from Jehoshaphat: (1 Kings 16:29-21:29)
    • Persecuted the prophets, promoted Baal worship, married Jezebel
    • Naboth's Vineyard (1 Kings 21) — Jezebel frames Naboth, has him stoned — Ahab takes the vineyard
    • 48 Laws Callback: Ahab is a 48-Laws operator before the book existed
  • Jehoshaphat Befriends Ahab (~1 min): Jehoshaphat allied with Ahab, hoping to end conflict between the two kingdoms. An admirable goal, but Ahab took advantage
    • Ahab's ask — "Will you go with me to battle at Ramoth-gilead?" Jehoshaphat's answer — "I am as you are, my people as your people, my horses as your horses." (1 Kings 22:4)
    • Jehoshaphat married his son Jehoram to Ahab's daughter Athaliah (2 Chronicles 18:1)
  • Jehoshaphat Does Not Belong in the Battle (~1 min):
    • God disciplines Israel/Judah by sending enemies to conquer them (Judges 2:14-15) — He was doing this to Ahab via Ben-hadad (1 Kings 20:1), but Jehoshaphat wades right into it
  • First Red Flags (~2 min): (1 Kings 22:5-28)
    • Ahab gathers 400 false prophets to give a thumbs up — Jehoshaphat knows they're fake
    • Jehoshaphat asks for a real prophet — enter Micaiah, who speaks God's truth but only ever speaks bad of Ahab
    • Micaiah says Ahab will die and the army will be scattered — jailed for the answer
    • God tried warning Jehoshaphat to stay out of it, but he did not listen.
  • Major Red Flag (~2 min): (1 Kings 22:29-33) Ahab proposes disguising himself while Jehoshaphat wears the royal robes, angling to get him killed in his place
    • The enemy realizes Jehoshaphat isn't the king of Israel and backs off — God saves him, while Ahab dies anyway, "by accident"
  • Don't Be Fooled (~2 min): It worked out fine for Jehoshaphat personally, but not for the people he loved most.
    • He married his son Jehoram to Ahab's daughter Athaliah, who later murders her own grandchildren to seize the throne (2 Kings 11:1)
    • Be careful of wolves — not just for you, but for those close to you.
  • Closing (~2 min): Dove means trusting God with the outcome; serpent means trusting yourself enough to think, plan, and act.
    • Jehoshaphat trusted God only; Ahab trusted himself only — both saw half the picture
    • Crossing-the-street analogy: don't refuse to cross out of fear — but still look both ways
Round 1
Are you more of a dove? Or a serpent?
Segment 2 — Wolves on the Outside  (~13 min | Neh 2, 4, 6)
  • Intro (~2 min): Hundreds of years after Jehoshaphat, Israel is besieged by Babylon — walls and temple destroyed, Jews taken into exile to Babylon
    • Persia conquers Babylon, lets the Jews go back home
    • Temple is rebuilt (Ezra)
    • Walls still destroyed, city in ruins
  • Who Is Nehemiah? (~2 min): Cupbearer to the king of Persia — real access and trust at the highest level of the empire
    • Beyond poison control, actual advisor (trusted barkeep)
    • Rubbed shoulders with real palace-level operators — already has experience with wolves
  • Who Are the Wolves? (~2 min): Sanballat (governs Samaria) and Tobiah (governs Ammon) — regional governors, not foreign kings
    • Confirmed even outside the Bible (Elephantine papyri)
    • Want Israel weak for their own gain (48 Laws) (Nehemiah 2:10)
    • Next to real imperial politics, small-time operators
  • Does Not Befriend Wolves (~3 min):
    • They mock him and the workers (Nehemiah 2:19-20; 4:1-3)
      • Doesn't retaliate, prays to God (Dove) (Nehemiah 4:4-5)
    • They set traps to try and ambush him (Nehemiah 6:2-4; 6:10-14)
      • Nehemiah ignores them and says go away (Serpent) (Nehemiah 6:3-4)
    • They start a rumor of rebellion (Nehemiah 6:5-9)
      • Doesn't argue — just says "they're making it up" (Serpent) (Nehemiah 6:8)
    • Not every attack needs a response — some are better off ignored.
  • Defense against Wolf Attacks (~3 min):
    • They plot to attack (Nehemiah 4:7-9)
      • Brings outside people in (Dove) (Nehemiah 4:22)
      • Arms civilians, keeps watch through the night (Serpent) (Nehemiah 4:13)
      • Keeps persistent vigil, "trowels in hand" (Serpent) (Nehemiah 4:16-18)
    • Not looking for a fight but prepared for one.
  • Closing (~1 min): Never retaliated — best revenge is success
    • Wall done in 52 days (Nehemiah 6:15-16)
    • Didn't play with wolves, but took the threat seriously
    • Later, Eliashib welcomes Tobiah into a room inside the temple itself (Nehemiah 13:4-9)
      • Nehemiah throws his stuff back out — that threat is managed better on the outside of the walls
    • Callback: Jehoshaphat welcomed a wolf into his family. This time, the wolf gets thrown back out.
Round 2
Do you know who the wolves in your life are? How are you treating them?
Segment 3 — Wolves on the Inside  (~15 min | Neh 1–2, 5)
  • Transition (~1 min): Sometimes the wolves are not outside the walls. Sometimes they are your boss or even family members
  • Wolf Boss: Artaxerxes (~4.5 min): Ruler living by the 48 Laws
    • Not a soft king — took the throne by crushing his father's assassin and rival brothers, then put down Egypt's revolt and his own general's rebellion. A real wolf
    • Takes time to prepare before going to the king (4 months) (Serpent) (Nehemiah 1:1; 2:1)
    • Shows vulnerability in showing sadness over Jerusalem (Dove) (Nehemiah 2:1-2)
      • Dangerous to be sad in front of the king — personal relationship allowed vulnerability
    • King asks "What do you need?" Nehemiah's preparation has him ready (Serpent)
      • A timeline, letters, timber request, and a role (Nehemiah 2:7-9)
      • Those same letters protect him later against the rebellion accusation
    • Preparedness is what faith looks like when you expect God to say yes.
  • Wolf Brothers: Jewish Nobility (~4.5 min): Should be living like sheep
    • On timing: likely addressed after the wall is built, but pressure built during wall construction
    • Four economic pressures on the poor:
      • Famine (Nehemiah 5:3)
      • The king's tax (Nehemiah 5:4)
      • City rebuilding (Nehemiah 5:1-2)
      • Nobles' interest (Nehemiah 5:7)
        • ~12% interest — exorbitant for the time and culture (Nehemiah 5:11)
        • Children taken as slaves to pay off debts (Nehemiah 5:5)
    • Wall rebuilding: beautiful cross-class community project, but this breaks it (Nehemiah 3)
      • Working alongside the guy you owe money to — or who owns your kid
  • Calling Wolf Brothers out to be Sheep (~4 min):
    • Nehemiah is angry, goes to God in prayer (Dove) (Nehemiah 5:6)
    • Calls a public assembly, as this was a community problem (Serpent) (Nehemiah 5:7)
      • Doesn't let them hide in secret
    • Other nations ARE wolves — God calls us not to be like them (Dove)
      • "We, according to our ability, have redeemed our Jewish brothers who have been sold to the nations, but even you sell your brothers, that they may be sold to us!" (Nehemiah 5:8)
    • Has them swear to the community AND the priests (Serpent) (Nehemiah 5:12-13)
    • Nehemiah was prepared in advance for this by his own conduct
      • Did not personally tax or exploit the poor, as was his right (Dove) (Nehemiah 5:14-15)
    • Good standing prepared him to directly correct his brothers' behavior.
  • Closing (~1 min):
    • He could not control how they would each respond, so he trusted God with that
    • Preparation helped him in both cases. Protected him in working with the King, and gave him a platform to speak to the Nobles
Round 3
When we work with wolves we need to be prepared. Is there something you are waiting on God for that you can prepare for? Or is there some sin in your life that will get in the way of correcting a brother?
Close — Live Like Sheep, But Not as Dumb as Them  (~5 min)
  • Two kingdoms, Judah and Israel — the two we still live inside
    • God's Kingdom: dove rules. World's: serpent rules (48 Laws)
    • Expect the conflict; be ready for it
  • Jehoshaphat wasn't ready — assumed good faith from a man who had none
    • Nehemiah was ready — kept his integrity, handled every wolf that came
    • Outside (Sanballat, Tobiah) or within his own people (the nobles)
  • The world says survive wolves by becoming one. Nehemiah proves that's not true.
  • Send-off: walking among wolves this week — ready to stay a sheep, and still protect your own?
