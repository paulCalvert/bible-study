Jesus: The Lion and the Lamb
What does it mean to be a man in both dimensions? Three sections: the picture of Jesus most of us have, what the real Jesus actually looked like (bold, angry, sharp-tongued AND gentle, sacrificial, compassionate), and what it looks like when the Holy Spirit works on a real man. ~32 men attended. Lively discussions. Very well received.

Section 1 — What Does Jesus Look Like to You?

5–10 min
Hook: Weird returning to Cedar Mill after 20 years. Same building, same faces (Jon Hamilton, Cameron Mierau). Changed for the better — Spanish church, women teaching. Familiarity + difference. Not a bad thing.

Transition: Jesus is the same way. You think you know him. Pull up that portrait — serene, calm, floating in sunlight. Familiar. But maybe not quite right.
The Problem With the Picture
  • The serene, glowing portrait — nice, calm, inoffensive
  • "I made myself look like this picture. And it felt fake. Jesus felt fake to me."
  • "Why do I need you? I can look like this on my own."
  • That picture began to fall apart — start of the testimony
  • We softened Jesus. We erased part of the example.
WWJD — The Real Answer
  • Be nice, non-offensive, a doormat?
  • Joke: Insult people. Flip tables. Whip people.
  • "No, that's not what it means…"
  • Reframe: More than that picture.
  • Lion AND Lamb — Rev 5:5–6 — Bold, confrontational, powerful AND gentle, sacrificial, compassionate
Table Questions — Round 1
1. What does Jesus look like to you?
2. How has that picture changed over the years?

Section 2 — Jesus as Lion and Lamb

15–18 min
Key passage: Revelation 5:5–6 — The only place in Scripture where Jesus is directly called the Lion of Judah — and immediately revealed as a slain Lamb. Both titles in the same vision. Same person.
The Lion — Anger
  • Anger feels ugly, can be destructive. How do we reconcile that with Jesus?
  • Two Stupid Angry Rules: Don't get angry over stupid things. Don't do stupid things when angry.
  • Temple (John 2:13–22) — blocking the command to Love God. Made a whip — deliberate, not impulsive. Anger + self-control.
  • Healing on the Sabbath (Mark 3:1–5) — blocking the command to Love People. "He looked around at them in anger… and healed the man."
  • Were these stupid reasons? No — barriers to the two greatest commands. Were these stupid actions? No — deliberate and self-controlled.
  • Angry at barriers. Comfortable with anger. Always self-controlled.
The Lion — Sharp Words
  • "If you have nothing nice to say…" — Jesus had a lot of ugly words.
  • Insults: hypocrites, snakes, vipers (Matt 23:13–33)
  • Sarcasm: "Have you not read?" — to men who had memorized the Torah. Like asking a doctor "Did you go to med school?"
  • Disciples: dim-witted, faithless, dense
  • Peter: Satan (Matt 16:23)
  • Same pattern throughout — tearing down pride, power, and ignorance that blocked people from God.
  • Who breaks down barriers — a Lamb, or a Lion?
The Lamb — Compassion, Sacrifice, Gentleness
  • Lion alone = toxic masculinity. We need both.
  • Compassion — Wept with Mary and Martha at Lazarus's tomb (John 11:28–35). Knew he was about to raise him. Still wept.
  • Sacrificial — Learned of John the Baptist's execution, tried to withdraw to grieve. Crowd followed. He set his grief aside and healed their sick (Matt 14:13–14).
  • Gentle — Woman caught in adultery (John 8:1–11). Could have condemned. Chose de-escalation. Protected her. "Neither do I condemn you."
  • True gentleness = strength restrained, not absent.
Not only Lion. Not only Lamb. Both. He wants us to be the same.
Table Questions — Round 2
1. Which of these traits — anger, sarcasm, or sharp words — do you see most in yourself? Do you think you're using it the way Jesus did?
2. Lion or Lamb — where do you feel out of balance right now, too harsh or too passive? What does that look like in your life?

Section 3 — What Does It Look Like for Us?

10 min + Close 5 min
Fear: if I give my life to Jesus, does everything change? Introvert → extrovert? Called to Uganda? That fear is valid — if God is that big, how can we not be completely changed? But: like coming back to CMBC — same + different. Surprised how much we don't change, just become more of what we already are.
Saul → Paul: The Lion Redirected
  • Before: Zealous, intense, educated, violent — dragged Christians from their homes (Acts 8–9)
  • Still Lion after: Calls out Peter publicly (Gal 2:11), missionary journeys, still had anger (Acts 23:3)
  • New Lamb side: "Least of the apostles" (1 Cor 15:9) · Relentless suffering for others (2 Cor 11:23–28) · Willing to be cut off from Christ for his enemies' sake (Rom 9:3)
  • → The intensity wasn't the problem. Where it was pointed was. Destroying → Building · Pride → Purpose · Self → Others
Timothy: The Lamb Made Bold
  • Before: Young, reserved, relational, faithful, obedient (Acts 16:3)
  • Called to Lion: "Do not be timid" (2 Tim 1:7) · Teach, correct, lead (1 Tim 4:11–16) · "Don't let anyone look down on you because you are young"
  • → Gentleness is not the absence of strength. It's strength under control.
  • Timid → Courageous · Follower → Leader · Quiet → Authoritative when needed
Table Questions — Round 3
What is one specific way God is calling you to grow — more bold or more gentle — and what would that look like this week?

Get Aligned
  • If you're a Lion: Pause before you speak. Listen before you react. Use your strength to protect, not to prove.
  • If you're a Lamb: Say the thing you've been avoiding. Step into the conversation. Take responsibility.
  • This isn't about personality — it's about who we are becoming.
  • Uncontrolled strength causes real damage. Unexercised strength leaves people unprotected.
  • They need you to be both.
Challenge
  • Look at the picture again — does it do Jesus justice?
  • This week: think about what Jesus really looks like.
  • One step toward becoming more like Him.
  • Jesus is good. Not always "nice." But good.
