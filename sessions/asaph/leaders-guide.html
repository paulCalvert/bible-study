Leader's Guide

Asaph: Worshipping God, Even When It's Hard

Event: CMBC Men's Breakfast  |  Date: November 7, 2026  |  Materials by: Paul Calvert  |  Scripture: ESV unless noted  |  Draft
Core Thread

Worship isn't a musical-aptitude test or a performance of having it all together. It's honest engagement with God, especially on the days we don't feel it.

The arc of the morning: worship that only reacts to God's victories (Section 1), worship that is appointed and shows up on schedule (Section 2), and what happens when the man whose job is praise can't find it (Section 3). The pattern to watch for: show up out of obligation → actually seek Him → meet Him → wonder.

Intro — Before Prayer  (~4 min)
1. Who Was Leading the Band? (~1.5 min)
  • Last month we watched David dance before the ark with everything he had. Michal watched from a window, and we spent some time on her side of that story.
  • But here's a question nobody asked last month: who was leading the band?
  • His name was Asaph.
2. Today (~1 min)
  • Asaph was the first man put in charge of Israel's worship music. Before we meet him, we're going to look at what worship sounded like before anyone had his job.
3. Worship Isn't Really for Me (~1 min)
  • This room is probably split on worship. Some of you connect with it naturally. Others hear "worship" and think, "That's not my lane." [Paul: personal example. The "worship isn't really for me" instinct, first person.]
4. Pray (~30 sec)
  • Pray, then move into Section 1.
Section 1 — When the Song Just Came Out  (~15 min | Exodus 14–15, 1 Samuel 1–2, Judges 5)
"Before anyone had Asaph's job, worship was a reaction. Something happened, and the song came out."
1. Before There Was a Worship Team (~1.5 min)
Genesis 4:21; Job 38:7
  • Music shows up early. Jubal was "the father of all those who play the lyre and pipe" (Genesis 4:21). When God laid the foundations of the earth, "the morning stars sang together" (Job 38:7). Hold onto that one. We'll come back to it at the very end.
  • But for centuries there was no choir, no rotation, no schedule. People sang when something had just happened.
2. The Red Sea (~4.5 min)
Exodus 14:11; 15:1-2, 20-21
  • Start with the complaint. Pharaoh's army is coming, and Israel turns on Moses: "Is it because there are no graves in Egypt that you have taken us away to die in the wilderness?" (Exodus 14:11)
  • Then the sea parts. And what's the first thing they do on the other side? Moses and the people sing (Exodus 15:1). Men are singing here. Not a choir, not the musical guys. Everybody.
    "The LORD is my strength and my song, and he has become my salvation." — Exodus 15:2
  • Then Miriam takes a tambourine, and all the women follow her out, dancing (Exodus 15:20). Listen to her line next to Moses':
    • Moses: "I will sing to the LORD, for he has triumphed gloriously" (15:1).
    • Miriam: "Sing to the LORD, for he has triumphed gloriously" (15:21).
  • One change. She takes a personal song and turns it into a call to everyone. She's the first worship leader in Scripture, and nobody appointed her.
  • And notice her title: "Miriam the prophetess" (15:20). Music and prophecy are linked from the very start. That will matter when we meet Asaph.
3. Don't Be Fooled: Three Days Later (~2 min)
Exodus 15:22-24
  • Three days into the wilderness, they find water, and it's bitter. "And the people grumbled against Moses, saying, 'What shall we drink?'" (Exodus 15:24)
  • Complaint. Then the greatest worship service in Israel's history. Then complaint again, inside of about a chapter.
  • Spontaneous worship is real. It's also got about a three-day shelf life.
  • (Don't moralize. Let it sit. Also notice, for later: they took their complaint to Moses.)
4. Hannah (~4.5 min)
1 Samuel 1:3-28; 2:1-10
  • Now something quieter. One woman instead of a nation.
  • Every year, Hannah's family goes up to Shiloh "to worship and to sacrifice to the LORD" (1 Samuel 1:3). It's the annual trip. It's on the calendar. And every year it's miserable for her. She's childless, and her rival provokes her about it. "So it went on year by year" (1:6-7). She cries and won't eat.
  • Her husband tries to help: "Am I not more to you than ten sons?" (1:8) (Let the room laugh.)
  • But she doesn't phone it in. She doesn't go through the motions of the trip and go home. She prays so hard that Eli the priest thinks she's drunk (1:13-14). Her answer:
    "I have been pouring out my soul before the LORD." — 1 Samuel 1:15
  • Samuel is born. And she sings on the day she hands him over at Shiloh (1:24-28; 2:1-10). Her worship came at the moment she let go.
  • One more detail for the guys who say they're not musical: the text says "Hannah prayed" (2:1). No instrument is mentioned.
  • The trip was an obligation she dreaded. She went anyway, and she actually sought God while she was there. Hold onto Hannah.
5. The Pattern (~1 min)
Judges 5; 1 Samuel 18:6-7
  • Quick roll call. Deborah and Barak sing after Sisera falls (Judges 5). The women come out singing after Goliath:
    "Saul has struck down his thousands, and David his ten thousands." — 1 Samuel 18:7
  • Notice who the crowd credits: David. We'll come back to that.
  • The sea parts, the enemy falls, the son is born, the giant drops, and then they sing. Every song comes after a victory.
6. Landing (~1.5 min)
  • Worship that runs on how we feel only shows up when we're winning.
  • Everybody's on their feet when the home team scores. The question is who's still in the stands in the fourth quarter, down by twenty.
  • Israel didn't have an answer for the fourth quarter. David did. His answer was to put it on the schedule.
Table Discussion — Round 1
When's the last time thankfulness to God just came out of you, unplanned? What had happened?
Follow-up
Israel went from singing to grumbling in three days. How long do your spiritual highs usually last, and what pulls you back down?
Section 2 — Worship Gets a Job Description  (~15 min | 2 Samuel 6, 1 Chronicles 13–16, 23, 25)
"David's answer to the three-day shelf life was appointed, scheduled, carefully done worship. And structure turned out not to be the enemy of heart."
1. Nobody Wanted to Be Near That Box (~3 min)
1 Samuel 5; 6:19; 2 Samuel 6:6-9; 1 Chronicles 13:3
  • Look at the ark's track record. The Philistines capture it, and their cities are struck (1 Samuel 5). It comes back, and seventy men of Beth-shemesh die for looking into it (1 Samuel 6:19). David's first attempt to bring it to Jerusalem, and Uzzah reaches out to steady it and dies on the spot (2 Samuel 6:6-7).
    "And David was afraid of the LORD that day, and he said, 'How can the ark of the LORD come to me?'" — 2 Samuel 6:9
  • And it had been sidelined a long time. "We did not seek it in the days of Saul" (1 Chronicles 13:3). Nobody alive had carried it the right way.
2. David Knows Who Killed Goliath (~2 min)
1 Samuel 17:47; 18:7
  • Remember the crowd's song: David's ten thousands. But listen to what David said before the fight: "the battle is the LORD's" (1 Samuel 17:47).
  • Everyone outside thinks that was all David. David knows it was God. And that same God is not someone you get casual with.
  • David is passionate and reverent. He knows God is dangerous, and he refuses to be overly familiar.
3. Doing It Right This Time (~2.5 min)
1 Chronicles 15:2, 12-19; 6:39-43
  • Second attempt. "No one but the Levites may carry the ark of God" (1 Chronicles 15:2). David owns the failure: the first time went wrong "because we did not seek him according to the rule" (15:13).
  • The Levites consecrate themselves and carry it on poles on their shoulders, "as Moses had commanded" (15:14-15).
  • Enter Asaph. He's appointed as one of the lead musicians, alongside Heman and Ethan (15:16-19). A Levite of the Gershonite clan, son of Berechiah (1 Chronicles 6:39-43).
  • Nobody in this procession is "feeling it" yet. They're focused on getting it right. This is worship as obligation.
4. Six Steps, Then the Turn (~3 min)
2 Samuel 6:13-14; 1 Chronicles 15:26-28
  • Picture the first steps. Everyone remembers Uzzah.
    "And when those who bore the ark of the LORD had gone six steps, he sacrificed an ox and a fattened animal." — 2 Samuel 6:13
  • Six steps, and nobody died. Then this: "because God helped the Levites who were carrying the ark," they sacrificed seven bulls and seven rams (1 Chronicles 15:26).
  • That's the encounter. They showed up out of obligation, they did it carefully, and God met them on the way.
  • And then the careful procession breaks wide open. "David danced before the LORD with all his might" (2 Samuel 6:14), with all Israel shouting, horns, trumpets, cymbals, harps and lyres (1 Chronicles 15:28).
  • It started with counting steps. It ended with leaps of joy.
  • (Michal gets a nod only: "You heard her side last month." Don't re-teach October.)
5. Worship Gets a Daily Shift (~2 min)
1 Chronicles 16:4, 37; 23:30; 25:1-2; 2 Chronicles 29:30
  • Once the ark is in place, Asaph gets his job description: "to invoke, to thank, and to praise the LORD" (1 Chronicles 16:4). "Invoke" means calling on God. Lament already has a place on the job description.
  • David leaves Asaph before the ark "to minister regularly before the ark as each day required" (16:37). Each day. Not each victory. The Levites stood "every morning, thanking and praising the LORD, and likewise at evening" (23:30).
  • And Asaph is called a "seer" (1 Chronicles 25:1-2; 2 Chronicles 29:30). Remember Miriam the prophetess. Music in Israel is proclamation, not performance.
6. The Lots (~1 min)
1 Chronicles 25:8
  • How were the duties assigned? By casting lots, "small and great, teacher and pupil alike" (1 Chronicles 25:8). Not by talent. Not by feeling.
  • You were on the schedule whether you felt musical, spiritual, or neither. "I'm not good at worship, so it's not my lane" doesn't survive this verse.
7. Landing (~1.5 min)
  • David didn't wait for Israel to feel like worshipping. He did it right, and the joy showed up on the way.
  • You don't go to practice because you feel like it. You go because it's Tuesday.
  • So here's the question. What happens when the guy whose whole job is praise shows up for his shift and can't do it?
Table Discussion — Round 2
What's kept you at arm's length from worship?
Follow-up
Is there anything you started out of obligation that you've come to love?
Section 3 — The Seer Who Couldn't See  (~15 min | Psalm 73, Job 1, 23, 42)
"Asaph, whose job was praise, nearly lost his faith. His post, the sanctuary, is where it turned."
1. He Tells You the Ending First (~1 min)
Psalm 73:1
  • Twelve psalms carry Asaph's name. He founded the guild, wrote several of them himself, and his descendants carried the tradition for generations. Psalm 73 is one of his.
  • It opens with the conclusion: "Truly God is good to Israel, to those who are pure in heart" (73:1). Then he tells you how he nearly lost it.
  • He's telling a story whose ending he already knows.
2. The Slip (~1.5 min)
Psalm 73:2-3
  • "But as for me, my feet had almost stumbled, my steps had nearly slipped. For I was envious of the arrogant when I saw the prosperity of the wicked." — Psalm 73:2-3
  • There's an old story about doctors arguing over what sharpens a man's eyesight. The winning answer: envy. It makes everything look bigger. (Thomas Le Blanc, quoted in Spurgeon, The Treasury of David)
3. The Complaint, and the Reveal (~2 min)
Psalm 73:4-13
  • Verses 4–12, summarized: the wicked are healthy, rich, proud, carefree, and nobody stops them. Spurgeon put it this way:
    "He ploughs least, and yet has the most fodder. He deserves the hottest hell, and yet has the warmest nest." — Spurgeon, The Treasury of David (KJV-based)
  • And then the low point: "All in vain have I kept my heart clean and washed my hands in innocence" (73:13).
  • Don't be fooled. We assume the worship guys have it figured out. This is the worship guy.
    "Asaph was a seer, but he could not see when reason left him in the dark." — Spurgeon, The Treasury of David
4. He Didn't Broadcast It (~2 min)
Psalm 73:15-16
  • "If I had said, 'I will speak thus,' I would have betrayed the generation of your children" (73:15). He's the man leading the songs. If he says this out loud, he tells everyone their faith is pointless.
  • But he couldn't carry it either: "it seemed to me a wearisome task" (73:16).
  • He didn't fake it, and he didn't broadcast it raw. He took it to God first.
  • Be careful what you broadcast, but don't carry it alone.
  • (Keep this balanced. In ten minutes the men will be asked to share honestly at their tables. This is not "keep your doubts to yourself.")
5. The Turn (~2.5 min)
Psalm 73:17-18
  • "...until I went into the sanctuary of God; then I discerned their end." — Psalm 73:17
  • The sanctuary was his post. The place David assigned him. Each day, as each day required. He went in on a day he didn't feel it, and he didn't just stand there. He went in, looking.
  • Spurgeon: "He shifted his point of view, and apparent disorder resolved itself into harmony." A musical picture, right at the turning point.
  • "Truly you set them in slippery places" (73:18). Spurgeon again: "If the wicked had not been raised so high they could not have fallen so low."
  • Remember Marah? Israel took its complaint to Moses. Asaph took his into the sanctuary. Same frustration, different destination.
6. Honest About Himself, and the Nevertheless (~2.5 min)
Psalm 73:21-26
  • "I was pricked in heart" (73:21). The older translations say "reins," meaning the kidneys, the gut. The modern word is gut-punched.
  • "I was brutish and ignorant; I was like a beast toward you" (73:22). The Hebrew word there is behemoth, the same word God uses in Job for the giant creature (Job 40:15). Hold onto Job.
  • And then the turn of the whole psalm:
    "Nevertheless, I am continually with you; you hold my right hand." — Psalm 73:23
  • Spurgeon notes these mercies were "given to one who confessed himself a fool," so "we also may hope to enjoy them." This is the line for the "worship's not for me" guy.
  • "Whom have I in heaven but you?" (73:25)
    "My flesh and my heart may fail, but God is the strength of my heart and my portion forever." — Psalm 73:26
  • (Don't rush the first half of verse 26. It will land with the older men in the room.)
7. When the Sanctuary Is Quiet: Job (~2 min)
Job 1:20-21; 23:3, 8-10; 38:7; 42:5
  • Now, honestly: not every shift ends with a sanctuary moment. Sometimes you show up, and you seek Him, and you can't find Him.
  • Job lost everything in a single day. His first response: he "fell on the ground and worshiped," and said, "The LORD gave, and the LORD has taken away; blessed be the name of the LORD" (Job 1:20-21). That's worship he did not feel.
  • And then he kept looking, for chapter after chapter:
    "Oh, that I knew where I might find him... Behold, I go forward, but he is not there, and backward, but I do not perceive him." — Job 23:3, 8
  • He kept going anyway: "But he knows the way that I take; when he has tried me, I shall come out as gold" (23:10).
  • The encounter did come. It just took until chapter 38. And when God finally speaks, He takes Job back to the morning the stars sang together (38:7), the verse we started with. Job's answer:
    "I had heard of you by the hearing of the ear, but now my eye sees you." — Job 42:5
  • Asaph's turn came when he walked into the sanctuary. Job's took thirty-seven chapters. Both of them kept showing up.
8. Landing (~1.5 min)
Psalm 73:28
  • "But for me it is good to be near God; I have made the Lord GOD my refuge, that I may tell of all your works." — Psalm 73:28
  • He shares the doubt only after taking it to God. And then he puts it in a song for the whole congregation to sing.
  • Spontaneous worship happens when you feel it. Organized worship carries you when you don't.
  • But it only carries you if you show up looking for Him.
  • Hannah on the yearly trip she dreaded. The Levites counting six steps. Asaph walking into the sanctuary on a day he'd nearly given up. Job, still looking in chapter 23. None of them felt it when they showed up. All of them actually sought Him while they were there. And for every one of them, obligation became encounter, and encounter became wonder.
  • So don't phone it in, and don't wait until you feel it. Worship was never about the instrument. It's showing up, paying attention, and being honest with God, including the gut-punched days.
Table Discussion — Round 3
Where do you actually take your doubts and frustrations with God?
Follow-up
What would it look like to show up anyway this month, on a week you don't feel it, and actually seek Him while you're there instead of just being present?
Close  (~4 min)
1. The Sons of Asaph (~1 min)
2 Chronicles 5:12; 29:13, 30; 35:15; Ezra 2:41; Nehemiah 7:44; 12:35, 46
  • Asaph's sons kept showing up too. They led worship for centuries: at the dedication of Solomon's temple, under Hezekiah, under Josiah, and among the exiles who came home from Babylon.
  • Last month was about legacy. Here's one: a family that kept showing up for its shift for five hundred years.
