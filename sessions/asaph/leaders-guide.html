Leader's Guide

Asaph: Worshipping God, Even When It's Hard

Event: CMBC Men's Breakfast  |  Date: November 7, 2026  |  Materials by: Paul Calvert  |  Scripture: ESV unless noted  |  Draft
Core Thread

Worship isn't a musical-aptitude test or a performance of having it all together. It's honest engagement with God, especially on the days we don't feel it.

The arc of the morning: worship that only reacts to God's victories (Section 1), worship that is appointed and shows up on schedule (Section 2), and what happens when the man whose job is praise can't find it (Section 3). The pattern to watch for: show up out of obligation → actually seek Him → meet Him → wonder.

Intro — Before Prayer  (~3.5 min)
1. Who Was Leading the Band? (~1.5 min)
  • Last month we watched David dance before the ark with everything he had. Michal watched from a window, and we spent some time on her side of that story.
  • But here's a question nobody asked last month: who was leading the band?
  • His name was Asaph.
2. Worship Isn't Really for Me (~1.5 min)
  • This room is probably split on worship. Some of you connect with it naturally. Others hear "worship" and think, "That's not my lane." [Paul: personal example. The "worship isn't really for me" instinct, first person.]
  • Years ago, when Cedar Mill's worship was a lot more reserved, Pastor Carl once told the congregation, half-joking: "If you feel so moved by the Spirit, you are allowed to raise your hands." [Paul: tell it firsthand.]
3. Pray (~30 sec)
  • Pray, then move into Section 1.
Section 1 — When the Song Just Came Out  (~10.5 min | Exodus 14–15, Numbers 21, Judges 5, 1 Samuel 18)
"Before anyone had Asaph's job, worship was a reaction. Something happened, and the song came out."
1. Before There Was a Worship Team (~30 sec)
  • For centuries there was no choir, no rotation, no schedule. People sang when something had just happened.
2. The Red Sea (~4 min)
Exodus 14:10-11, 21-22; 15:1-2, 20-21
  • Quick setup, because most of you know this one. Israel is finally free from Egypt, but Pharaoh changes his mind and sends his army after them. They're pinned against the sea, and they cry out to the LORD (Exodus 14:10). They also turn on Moses: "Is it because there are no graves in Egypt that you have taken us away to die in the wilderness?" (Exodus 14:11)
  • And God saves them, in about the most dramatic way possible. He parts the Red Sea, and they walk through on dry ground with a wall of water on either side (14:21-22). They go in terrified. They come out the other side singing.
  • Moses and all Israel sing (Exodus 15:1-18). And listen to what they sing about. Not how they feel. What God just did:
    "I will sing to the LORD, for he has triumphed gloriously; the horse and his rider he has thrown into the sea. The LORD is my strength and my song, and he has become my salvation." — Exodus 15:1-2
  • And men are singing here. Not a choir, not the musical guys. Everybody. Some of us should probably sing quietly. But we should still sing.
  • Moses sings, "I will sing to the LORD." Then Miriam picks up a tambourine, the women follow her, dancing, and she turns it into a call to action: "Sing to the LORD, for he has triumphed gloriously" (15:20-21).
  • God showed up, and God won. The song is the response.
3. The Pattern (~1.5 min)
Numbers 21:16-17; Judges 5:1-3; 1 Samuel 18:6-7
  • And it's not just the Red Sea. Over and over, God shows up and wins, and praise follows:
    • God gives Israel water in the desert, and they sing to the well: "Spring up, O well! Sing to it!" (Numbers 21:16-17)
    • God routs Sisera's army, and Deborah and Barak sing: "to the LORD I will sing; I will make melody to the LORD, the God of Israel" (Judges 5:1-3).
    • David kills Goliath, and the women come out of every town singing and dancing (1 Samuel 18:6-7):
      "Saul has struck down his thousands, and David his ten thousands." — 1 Samuel 18:7
  • The sea parts, the water flows, the enemy falls, the giant drops, and then they sing. Every song comes after a win.
4. What's Wrong With This Model? (~1 min)
  • So what's wrong with this kind of worship? Nothing. It's real, and it's right. God acted, and His people praised Him.
  • It's just not sustainable. What happens when God goes quiet?
5. Don't Be Fooled: Three Days Later (~2 min)
Exodus 15:22-24
  • Go back to that crowd on the shore of the Red Sea. Three days into the wilderness, they find water, and it's bitter. "And the people grumbled against Moses, saying, 'What shall we drink?'" (Exodus 15:24)
  • Complaint. Then the greatest worship service in Israel's history. Then complaint again, inside of about a chapter.
  • Spontaneous worship is real. It's also got about a three-day shelf life.
  • (Don't moralize. Let it sit. Also notice, for later: they took their complaint to Moses.)
6. Landing (~1.5 min)
  • Worship that runs on how we feel only shows up when we're winning.
  • Everybody's on their feet when the home team scores. The question is who's still in the stands in the fourth quarter, down by twenty.
  • David knew about God's victories, and he certainly sang about them (2 Samuel 22). He also knew about failure and God's silence (Psalm 13:1), and that we need to sing through those times too.
  • Israel didn't have an answer for the fourth quarter. David did. His answer was to put it on the schedule.
Table Discussion — Round 1
When's the last time thankfulness to God just came out of you, unplanned? What had happened?
Follow-up
Israel went from singing to grumbling in three days. How long do your spiritual highs usually last, and what pulls you back down?
To consider: "Have you ever misjudged how someone else connects with God, or been misjudged yourself?"
Section 2 — Worship Gets a Job Description  (~17 min | 1 Samuel 5–7, 2 Samuel 5–6, Numbers 4, 1 Chronicles 6, 13–16, 23, 25)
"David's answer to the three-day shelf life was appointed, scheduled, carefully done worship. And structure turned out not to be the enemy of heart."
1. David Wants the Ark Home (~1 min)
2 Samuel 5:6-7; 6:1-2; 1 Samuel 7:1-2; 1 Chronicles 13:3
  • David is king now. He's taken Jerusalem and made it his city (2 Samuel 5:6-7).
  • But the ark of the covenant, the place where God's presence rested among His people, isn't there. It's been sitting in a private house in Kiriath-jearim for at least twenty years (1 Samuel 7:1-2). "We did not seek it in the days of Saul" (1 Chronicles 13:3).
  • David wants it in Jerusalem. But there's a problem. What is it?
2. The Ark's Body Count (~4 min)
1 Samuel 5:1-12; 6:19-20; 2 Samuel 6:3-12
  • Dagon. The Philistines capture the ark in battle and set it in the temple of their god, Dagon. The next morning, Dagon is lying face down in front of the ark. They stand him back up. The next morning he's face down again, with his head and both hands broken off on the threshold (1 Samuel 5:1-4).
  • Three Philistine cities. The LORD strikes Ashdod with tumors (5:6). They move the ark to Gath: tumors there too (5:9). They move it to Ekron, and the people scream, "They have brought around to us the ark of the God of Israel to kill us and our people" (5:10). "There was a deathly panic throughout the whole city" (5:11). The Philistines send it back.
  • Beth-shemesh. The ark comes home on a cart. The men of Beth-shemesh look into it, and God strikes seventy of them (6:19). Their response:
    "Who is able to stand before the LORD, this holy God?" — 1 Samuel 6:20
  • Uzzah. David's first attempt. They put the ark on a new cart, the same way the Philistines sent it back (2 Samuel 6:3). The oxen stumble, Uzzah reaches out to steady it, and God strikes him dead on the spot (6:6-7).
  • After that, David leaves the ark at the house of a man named Obed-edom, where it stays for three months before he tries again (6:10-12).
  • That's the problem. The ark is the presence of a holy God, and you don't handle Him however you like.
3. Doing It Right This Time (~1 min)
1 Chronicles 15:2, 12-17; Numbers 4:15
  • Second attempt. "No one but the Levites may carry the ark of God" (1 Chronicles 15:2). David owns the failure: the first time went wrong "because we did not seek him according to the rule" (15:13).
  • And notice: the Levites haven't done this in a long time. The ark sat in one house for at least twenty years and through all of Saul's reign. Nobody alive had carried it. They're going back to instructions written down in Moses' day (Numbers 4:15).
  • The Levites consecrate themselves and carry it on poles on their shoulders, "as Moses had commanded" (1 Chronicles 15:14-15). No cart this time.
  • And David tells the chiefs of the Levites to appoint singers and musicians for the procession. One of the men they appoint, alongside Heman and Ethan, is named Asaph (1 Chronicles 15:16-17).
  • Nothing about this is casual.
4. Enter Asaph (~2 min)
1 Chronicles 6:39-43; Numbers 4:24-26; 1 Chronicles 15:19; 16:5, 7
  • Asaph is a Levite, the son of Berechiah, from the clan of Gershon. His family line runs all the way back to Gershom, son of Levi (1 Chronicles 6:39-43).
  • His family's chore: in the wilderness, the Gershonites carried the tabernacle's curtains, coverings, screens and cords (Numbers 4:24-26). They were the movers.
  • His instrument: bronze cymbals (15:19). Before long he's the chief musician before the ark (16:5), and the first man David hands a song of thanks to (16:7).
  • Israel's first appointed worship leader came from a family that hauled tent curtains.
5. Everyone at Ground Zero (~2 min)
1 Chronicles 15:25-27; 2 Samuel 6:9
  • Picture the scene before the first step. The elders and the commanders. The Levites with the poles on their shoulders. The singers in fine linen robes, Chenaniah leading the music, Asaph with his cymbals, and David himself in a linen robe (1 Chronicles 15:25-27). [Paul: paint the picture.]
  • Nobody here is "feeling it." They had all heard the stories of Uzzah and Beth-shemesh. And David? Remember what he said the last time he tried this:
    "And David was afraid of the LORD that day, and he said, 'How can the ark of the LORD come to me?'" — 2 Samuel 6:9
    Three months later, he's standing at the front of the procession, scared of getting it wrong again.
  • This is worship as obligation. They're not focused on feeling anything. They're focused on getting it right.
6. Six Steps. Sacrifice. Repeat. (~1.5 min)
2 Samuel 6:13; 1 Chronicles 15:26
  • "And when those who bore the ark of the LORD had gone six steps, he sacrificed an ox and a fattened animal." — 2 Samuel 6:13
  • Six steps, and nobody died. Sacrifice. Six more steps. Sacrifice. Lather, rinse, repeat. (Interpretive: the verse can be read as one sacrifice after the first six steps, or a sacrifice every six steps. Readers differ. Present it as one reading.)
  • And then this: "because God helped the Levites who were carrying the ark," they sacrificed seven bulls and seven rams (1 Chronicles 15:26).
  • They showed up out of obligation, they did it carefully, and God met them on the way.
7. Structured Worship Becomes Natural Worship (~1 min)
2 Samuel 6:14-15; 1 Chronicles 15:28
  • Somewhere along the way, they stopped counting steps, and they began dancing. "David danced before the LORD with all his might" (2 Samuel 6:14), with all Israel shouting, horns, trumpets, cymbals, harps and lyres (1 Chronicles 15:28).
  • It started with counting steps. It ended with leaps of joy.
8. Worship Gets a Daily Shift, Assigned by Lot (~3 min)
2 Samuel 6:20-23; 1 Chronicles 16:4, 37; 23:1, 30; 25:8
  • All in all, Asaph had a good day! We know David has a marital spat when he gets home (2 Samuel 6:20-23), but as for Asaph, he's still alive! And it was a rockin' worship service!
  • On top of that, he got a permanent promotion. Once the ark is in place, Asaph gets his job description: "to invoke, to thank, and to praise the LORD" (1 Chronicles 16:4). "Invoke" means calling on God. Lament already has a place on the job description. And David leaves Asaph before the ark "to minister regularly before the ark as each day required" (16:37). Each day. Not each victory.
  • And it didn't stop there. Years later, near the end of his life, David organized the whole operation (1 Chronicles 23:1). The Levites stood "every morning, thanking and praising the LORD, and likewise at evening" (23:30). And how were the duties assigned? By casting lots, "small and great, teacher and pupil alike" (1 Chronicles 25:8). Not by talent. Not by feeling. [Paul: drawing-straws moment, a few men draw for roles.]
  • You were on the schedule whether you felt musical, spiritual, or neither. "I'm not good at worship, so it's not my lane" doesn't survive this verse.
9. Landing (~1.5 min)
  • David didn't wait for Israel to feel like worshipping. He put it on a schedule, to help the people worship even on the bad days, or the boring ones, when there's no victory to celebrate.
  • You don't go to practice because you feel like it. You go because it's Tuesday.
  • Worship can start as an obligation. Keep showing up, and somewhere along the way, it becomes real.
Table Discussion — Round 2
Is there anything you started out of obligation that you've come to love?
Follow-up
What keeps you showing up with God on the boring weeks, when nothing's going wrong and nothing's going right?
To consider: "What's kept you at arm's length from worship?"
Section 3 — The Seer Who Couldn't See  (~12 min | Psalm 73)
"Asaph, whose job was praise, nearly lost his faith. His post, the sanctuary, is where it turned."
1. He Tells You the Ending First (~1 min)
Psalm 73:1
  • So here's the question. What happens when the guy whose whole job is praise shows up for his shift and can't do it?
  • Twelve psalms carry Asaph's name. He founded the guild, wrote several of them himself, and his descendants carried the tradition for generations. Psalm 73 is one of his.
  • It opens with the conclusion: "Truly God is good to Israel, to those who are pure in heart" (73:1). Then he tells you how he nearly lost it.
  • He's telling a story whose ending he already knows.
2. The Slip, and the Reveal (~3 min)
Psalm 73:2-13; 1 Chronicles 25:1-2; 2 Chronicles 29:30; Exodus 15:20
  • "But as for me, my feet had almost stumbled, my steps had nearly slipped. For I was envious of the arrogant when I saw the prosperity of the wicked." — Psalm 73:2-3
  • Verses 4–12, summarized: the wicked are healthy, rich, proud, carefree, and nobody stops them. Spurgeon put it this way:
    "He ploughs least, and yet has the most fodder. He deserves the hottest hell, and yet has the warmest nest." — Spurgeon, The Treasury of David (KJV-based)
  • And then the low point: "All in vain have I kept my heart clean and washed my hands in innocence" (73:13).
  • And remember who's writing this. Asaph isn't just the band leader. Scripture calls him a "seer" (2 Chronicles 29:30), and says he and his sons "prophesied" with lyres, harps and cymbals (1 Chronicles 25:1-2). Miriam, leading the women's song at the Red Sea, is called a prophetess too (Exodus 15:20). Along with leading worship, the job was proclaiming God to the people.
  • Don't be fooled. We assume the worship guys have it figured out. This is the worship guy, the seer, and he couldn't see it.
    "Asaph was a seer, but he could not see when reason left him in the dark." — Spurgeon, The Treasury of David
3. He Didn't Broadcast It (~2 min)
Psalm 73:15-16
  • "If I had said, 'I will speak thus,' I would have betrayed the generation of your children" (73:15). He's the man leading the songs. If he says this out loud, he tells everyone their faith is pointless.
  • But he couldn't carry it either: "it seemed to me a wearisome task" (73:16).
  • He didn't fake it, and he didn't broadcast it raw. He took it to God first.
  • Be careful what you broadcast, but don't carry it alone.
4. Until... (~2.5 min)
Psalm 73:16-18
  • Stop on one word: "until." It's a small word, but it means something is about to change. Something happens, and things turn out different than you thought. Everything Asaph has been building up for sixteen verses is about to be turned on its head.
  • "...until I went into the sanctuary of God; then I discerned their end." — Psalm 73:17
  • The sanctuary was his post. The place David assigned him. Each day, as each day required. He went in on a day he didn't feel it, and he didn't just stand there. He went in, looking.
  • Spurgeon: "He shifted his point of view, and apparent disorder resolved itself into harmony." A musical picture, right at the turning point.
  • "Truly you set them in slippery places" (73:18).
  • The prosperity Asaph envied turns out to be the trap. God set them on that smooth ground, and it's how they fall to ruin, "destroyed in a moment" (73:18-19). What looked like a blessing is really a burden. And God doesn't leave the ones He loves standing on it alone. Asaph's own feet "had nearly slipped" (73:2), but a few verses later, God is holding his right hand (73:23).
  • Remember Marah? Israel took its complaint to Moses. Asaph took his into the sanctuary. Same frustration, different destination.
5. Nevertheless (~2 min)
Psalm 73:21-26
  • Now that he can see again, Asaph looks back at himself honestly. Bitter, gut-punched, "brutish and ignorant... like a beast toward you" (73:21-22). Not a flattering picture.
  • And then he re-evaluates everything:
    "Nevertheless, I am continually with you; you hold my right hand. You guide me with your counsel, and afterward you will receive me to glory." — Psalm 73:23-24
    God never let go of him, even while he was envying, complaining and acting like an animal. Spurgeon notes these mercies were "given to one who confessed himself a fool," so "we also may hope to enjoy them."
  • He walked in envying what they had. Now look at what he wants:
    "Whom have I in heaven but you? And there is nothing on earth that I desire besides you. My flesh and my heart may fail, but God is the strength of my heart and my portion forever." — Psalm 73:25-26
  • They have the prosperity. He has God. And he wouldn't trade.
6. Landing (~1.5 min)
Psalm 73:28
  • "But for me it is good to be near God; I have made the Lord GOD my refuge, that I may tell of all your works." — Psalm 73:28
  • He shares the doubt only after taking it to God. And then he puts it in a song for the whole congregation to sing.
  • The Levites counting six steps. Asaph walking into the sanctuary on a day he'd nearly given up. Neither of them felt it when they showed up. Both of them actually sought Him while they were there. And for both of them, obligation became encounter, and encounter became wonder.
  • So don't phone it in, and don't wait until you feel it. Worship was never about the instrument. It's showing up, paying attention, and being honest with God, including the gut-punched days.
  • Showing up only matters if you show up looking for Him.
  • Spontaneous worship happens when you feel it. Organized worship carries you when you don't.
Table Discussion — Round 3
Where do you actually take your doubts and frustrations with God?
Follow-up
What would it look like to show up anyway this month, on a week you don't feel it, and actually seek Him while you're there instead of just being present?
Close  (~5.5 min)
1. The Sons of Asaph (~1 min)
2 Chronicles 5:12; 29:13, 30; 35:15; Ezra 2:41; Nehemiah 7:44; 12:35, 46
  • Asaph's sons kept showing up too. They led worship for centuries: at the dedication of Solomon's temple, under Hezekiah, under Josiah, and among the exiles who came home from Babylon.
  • Last month was about legacy. Here's one: a family that kept showing up for its shift for five hundred years.
2. When the Sanctuary Is Quiet: Job (~1.5 min)
Job 1:20-21; 23:3, 8-10; 42:5
  • Some of you are thinking, "I show up. I look for Him. And I still can't find Him." Not every shift ends with a sanctuary moment.
  • Job lost everything in a single day. His first response: he "fell on the ground and worshiped," and said, "The LORD gave, and the LORD has taken away; blessed be the name of the LORD" (Job 1:20-21). That's worship he did not feel.
  • And then he kept looking, chapter after chapter:
    "Oh, that I knew where I might find him... Behold, I go forward, but he is not there, and backward, but I do not perceive him." — Job 23:3, 8
    He kept going anyway: "But he knows the way that I take; when he has tried me, I shall come out as gold" (23:10).
  • The encounter did come. It just took until chapter 38. Job's answer:
    "I had heard of you by the hearing of the ear, but now my eye sees you." — Job 42:5
  • Asaph's turn came when he walked into the sanctuary. Job's took thirty-seven chapters. Both of them kept showing up.
3. Responsive Reading (~1.5 min)
Ezra 3:10-11; Psalm 73:23-26
  • When the temple foundation was rebuilt, "the Levites, the sons of Asaph, with cymbals" led the praise, "and they sang responsively," one group answering another (Ezra 3:10-11). Let's do that with Asaph's own words. I'll read a line, and you read the next one together.
  • Leader reads the first line, the room reads the second, alternating.

    Leader Nevertheless, I am continually with you; you hold my right hand.

    All You guide me with your counsel, and afterward you will receive me to glory.

    Leader Whom have I in heaven but you? And there is nothing on earth that I desire besides you.

    All My flesh and my heart may fail, but God is the strength of my heart and my portion forever.

4. Pray (~1.5 min)
  • Close in prayer.
