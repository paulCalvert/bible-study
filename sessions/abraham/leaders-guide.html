Abraham: Growing in Trust — A Lifetime of Walking with God

Event: CMBC Men's Breakfast  |  Date: July 2026  |  Materials by: Paul Calvert
Session Theme How does a man learn to walk with God? Abraham is our model — not because he was great, but because he kept walking. Through failure, growth, and decades of experience, he grew to trust God completely. This is a story about how great God is.

Audience: Broadly applicable — every man knows what it is to wait, to fail, and to wonder if God is still there.
Intro — Before Prayer  (~2 min)
Hook the room before you open in prayer.
1. What Do We Know? (~30 sec)
  • Ask the room: "What do we know about Abraham?"
2. Why He Matters (~30 sec)
  • Abraham is important. Two of the biggest faiths in the world are Abrahamic faiths — Judaism and Islam both trace their roots directly to him. Christianity does too, through a different branch. This is not a minor figure.
3. Bigger Than We Have Time For (~30 sec)
  • His story is incredible. This morning we will go over his life in highlights — there is far more than we have time for. Read through it on your own using the take-home notes.
4. Not Remarkable (~30 sec)
  • The story of Abraham is remarkable because Abraham himself is NOT remarkable. He is just a guy trying to figure out how to be friends with God — without a Bible, without a godly family, without brothers in the faith. He had none of that. Just a guy and God.
5. Boice — Then Pray
  • "Although we cannot be lawgivers, kings, generals, or prophets, we can be what Abram was — a man who heard God and believed that God can be trusted to do what he says he will do, and based his entire life on that conviction." — Boice
  • Let that land. Open in prayer, then move into Section 1.
Section 1 — Learning More About God  (~13 min)
We are going to walk through Abraham's story from the beginning — not to give a biography, but to watch how a man learns to trust God over a lifetime.
1. Setting the Scene (~2 min)
Genesis 11
  • We first meet him in Genesis 11. His name is Abram — not Abraham yet. We will get there.
  • He is in Ur, pre-Babylon. Babylon later becomes the center of power and wealth in that world. Abram got in before the prices shot up. He appears to have come from money and privilege.
  • Close-knit family: living with his dad Terah, married to Sarai, close with his nephew Lot.
  • He had it all: house, family, money, and a beautiful wife.
    • One thing missing: no kids. In our culture that is not a big deal. Back then it was a very big deal — children were your legacy, your security, your future.
    • Abram and Sarai are still relatively new in their marriage — but a few years in, they are likely starting to wonder if it is going to happen for them.
2. The Call (~2 min)
Genesis 12:1–2
  • God shows up and speaks to Abram. He speaks directly into Abram's deepest fear — and promises him children.
  • But He is also calling Abram to do something very difficult and culturally unnatural: leave his home, his people, and his family.
    • In America we value striking off on your own, leaving the nest. Back then you did not do that. Your identity, your security, your future — all of it flowed from your clan.
  • God was calling Abram to stop trusting in his house, his culture, even his family — and to trust in Him alone.
3. Haran: Half-Obedience (~3 min)
Genesis 11:31, 12:1
  • How does Abram respond? He half-obeys God. He leaves Ur but brings his father Terah and his nephew Lot. He gets stuck in Haran for fifty years — his father was too old to keep traveling.
    • (For more on Abram being called twice and the Haran timeline, it is in the notes. We do not have time to cover it all this morning.)
  • Terah dies. God calls Abram again. This time Abram follows God.
  • Learns: God is patient and wants us to come back to him.
  • How many of us have said something similar? "God, once my career takes off, then I will have time for you." "Once I meet the right girl, then I will figure it out." "God, I swear, once March Madness is over, then I will make time for you."
4. A Good Season (~1 min)
Genesis 12:6–9
  • Abram is in Canaan. He is no longer settling in — he is following where God leads, moving as God calls him.
  • Everywhere he stops, he builds an altar. He is learning to listen to and follow God, worshipping as he goes, trusting Him step by step.
5. Egypt (~5 min)
Genesis 12:10–20
  • Then famine hits. Abram could have asked God for help — instead, he looks to Egypt.
  • Sarai is beautiful — it is in the text. Abram is genuinely afraid the Egyptians will kill him to take her. So he tells Sarai to say she is his sister. Technically half-true — they were half-siblings. He thinks he can manage the situation.
    • He does not have it under control. As soon as they arrive, Sarai is immediately taken into Pharaoh's palace.
  • Has Abram screwed up everything? How can he be the father of a nation if his wife is gone?
  • God is faithful to Abram anyway. Disease on Pharaoh's household. Pharaoh figures it out, returns Sarai, pays Abram off, and kicks them out.
    • Abram returns to Bethel — back to where he last had a real relationship with God — and worships Him there. (Genesis 13:3–4)
  • Learns: God doesn't fail His promises even when Abram does.
Table Discussion — Round 1
Choose one:
What have you said you need to take care of before you make time for God?
— or —
Have you walked into a bad decision thinking you had it under control? How did that go?
Section 2 — Two Steps Forward, One Step Back  (~13 min)
Abram is back in Canaan, following God and trusting Him. But he is about to discover that the walk does not get easier just because it is going well.
1. Bridge — Aftermath of Egypt (~2 min)
Genesis 13
  • Abram is back in Canaan, following God. But there is a new problem — a leftover from Egypt. He has too much money.
  • Lot picks the lush Jordan Valley. Lot camps near Sodom. He is thinking about Lot, not about God.
  • Abram, I think, after Egypt had made a decision: "I trust God now. Even if I end up in the desert, God will be there with me." Lot's wealth owned him. Abram owned his wealth.
  • Growth: Abram is trusting God to provide instead of the world.
2. War, Rescue, and the King of Sodom (~3 min)
Genesis 14
  • War breaks out. Lot is carried off with the plunder. Abram rides out, wins, and gets everything back.
    • (Battle details — I would love to get into it. It is all in the notes. No time today.)
    • (Melchizedek — we definitely do not have time. It is in the notes. It is worth reading.)
  • The King of Sodom: "Give me my people back and you can keep the loot." Abram turns it down flat.
    • What Pharaoh had done still irked him. He was not going to let a king take credit for what God had done. He wanted God to get the credit for his blessings.
  • Growth: Abram is trusting God to provide instead of the world.
3. The Hinge (~4 min)
Genesis 15
  • After the battle, God speaks to Abram. But Abram has one thing on his mind.
    • Genesis 15:2 — "Lord God, what will you give me, for I continue childless?"
  • God says: don't look down. Look up. Count the stars. So shall your offspring be.
  • Then — possibly the most important verse in the entire Bible:
    • Genesis 15:6 — "Abram believed God, and it was credited to him as righteousness."
    • This is the hinge of salvation. Paul quotes it in Romans and Galatians. James quotes it. NT writers keep coming back to it. The foundation of how God saves anyone — not performance, not achievement. Just belief.
  • Learns: God can be trusted.
  • (The covenant ceremony — God alone passes through the pieces. The whole weight of the promise rests on God. Extraordinary. Read it in the notes.)
4. Hagar — Even After the Hinge (~4 min)
Genesis 16
  • You might be thinking: alright, he has got this now. He doesn't.
  • Sarai is despairing. She recommends Abram have children through her servant Hagar.
    • This was not unusual culturally. Servants' children were legally considered the wife's.
    • Personal note: my wife and I had difficulty having children — three years of trying. We were desperate. Abram and Sarai have been dealing with this for over sixty years. I understand the desperation.
  • Hagar gets pregnant.
    • (Confused yay wave.)
    • Now Sarai knows she is the problem. Rivalry erupts. Hagar gets driven out while still pregnant.
  • God stepped into an incredibly messy, completely unnecessary situation and redeemed it.
  • Learns: God doesn't need us to solve the problems he said he will solve.
Table Discussion — Round 2
Lead:
Two steps forward, one step back. Where do you see that pattern in your own walk with God?
Section 3 — Walking Steadier, But Not Perfect  (~13 min)
1. Circumcision + The Name (~5 min)
Genesis 17:1–27
  • Twenty nine years since the promise. God gives Abraham a new sign of the covenant — circumcision.
  • Genesis 17:23 — that very day, Abraham did it. No delay, no negotiation.
    • Compare that to Haran. The man who spent 40–50 years at a rest stop now moves immediately when God speaks.
  • God gave Abram a new name: Abraham — "father of multitudes." At 99 years old with no son by Sarah.
    • Shaq story.
  • Growth: Abram obeys God immediately and fully.
2. Abimelech (~5 min)
Genesis 20
  • Abraham is on an upward trajectory. And he fumbles — the same mistake as Egypt. Passes Sarah off as his sister.
  • God protects Sarah again. Appears to Abimelech in a dream.
    • Abimelech paid Abraham off. No moral ground to refuse.
  • Same lesson twice. God was patient with Abraham. He is patient with us.
  • Learns: I can fail in the same ways again and again, and God will still take me back.
3. Isaac (~3 min)
Genesis 21:1–7
  • And then — God kept every word He had spoken.
  • Three phrases in two verses: "as he had said," "what he had promised," "at the very time God had promised."
    • Genesis 21:1–2
  • Human doubt, human schemes, human failure — none of it moved the promise or delayed the delivery.
  • He failed early. He failed late. He failed after his biggest growth moments. But he kept walking. And God kept every word.
  • Learns: God keeps his promises.
Table Discussion — Round 3
Lead:
What are some things you think you should be better at or know better by now?
Optional follow-up:
Has God been patient with you in that area — shown up anyway even when you kept stumbling?
Close — The Sacrifice of Isaac  (~5 min)
Genesis 22
  • After everything. Decades of waiting, failures, growth. Isaac finally in his arms — the promise fulfilled.
  • And God asks for it all back.
    • Genesis 22:2 — "Take your son, your only son Isaac, whom you love, and offer him."
  • Not blind faith — 60–70 years of evidence. Abraham would have failed this earlier in his walk.
  • The whole morning has been about one thing: how does a man grow to trust God?
    • Abraham's final exam was simple: "Do you trust me with all of it?"
    • He said yes — because everything had taught him God could be trusted with everything.
  • God stopped him. Delivered a ram. Kept His word one more time.
  • This was never a story about how great Abraham was — it is a story about how great God is.
