Section 1 — David's Legacy  (~15 min, rehearsed | 1–2 Samuel)
  • [S6 · 0:00] The Contrast Figure (~45 sec) — (1 Sam 13:14; Acts 13:22) obscure → let's start with someone who isn't. Man after God's own heart. Full David series next year, not today
    • Landing: legacy with God, remarkable. Legacy with family, a tragedy
  • [S7 · ~0:45] Michal and the Ark (~1.25 min) — first wife, Saul's daughter (1 Sam 18:20-27). Ark into Jerusalem, David dances, Michal despises from window (2 Sam 6:16)
    • Read her rebuke (6:20 NIV): "How the king of Israel has distinguished himself today..."
    • Read the famous part only (6:21-22 NIV): "...I will celebrate before the LORD. I will become even more undignified than this, and I will be humiliated in my own eyes..."
  • [~2:00] The Usual Lesson, and Her Side (~30 sec) — "be like David, not Michal" (rhetorical, no answers). Sermons dunk on her. Let's read it from her side
  • [S8–9 · ~2:30] Michal's Story (~3.5 min, let it breathe)
    • S8 Love story: Michal loved David (18:20), Jonathan = best friend (18:1-4), giant-killer, likely next king
    • S9 Isolation: Saul turns, window escape (19:11-17), Saul turns on Jonathan (20:30-33), given to Paltiel (25:44)
    • Loss: father + 3 brothers die in one battle (31:2-6). Ish-bosheth, civil war (2 Sam 2:10). Loyalties torn in half
    • Separation: Abner deal (3:13-14). Paltiel weeping all the way to Bahurim (3:15-16)
  • [S10 · 6:00] Happily Reunited!... Right? (~2 min) — walking to David. Excited? Scared? 10-15 years
    • Sees him: older, handsome, BUT RUGGED. Heart rushes, goes to speak... WHO THE HECK ARE ALL THESE WOMEN?
      • 6 wives: Ahinoam, Abigail, Maacah, Haggith, Abital, Eglah (3:2-5). She's #7
    • Then MORE wives/concubines, no count. After reunion, before the Ark (5:13)
    • Landing: married to a man she no longer knows, competing for him, old family dying off. NO ONE ELSE LEFT: father dead, brothers dead, no kids. David = her only lifeline. She's looking at a stranger
  • [S11 · 8:00] Revisiting the Ark Scene (~30 sec)
    • Back to the Ark, knowing what we know now: first love ripped away, given to another man, father + brothers killed, taken back in a political deal, house full of wives
    • Michal: hurt + isolated. Worship is deeply personal, doesn't look the same for everyone → sees worship she can't connect with → lashes out. Not right, but I understand
    • Landing: could have brought them closer. So what does David do with it?
  • [S12 · 8:30] David's Response (~2 min)
    • Responds poorly. "Even more undignified" = not the problem. Make it a poster
    • Read the WHOLE response:
      "It was before the LORD, who chose ME rather than YOUR FATHER or ANYONE FROM HIS HOUSE... But by THESE SLAVE GIRLS you spoke of, I WILL BE HELD IN HONOR." 2 Samuel 6:21-22 (NIV)
    • Problem = everything around it. Chose me over your just-killed family. Servants' opinion > yours. Tone: "Maybe you could learn something from them, Michal!" David honored, Michal left behind
    • 6:23 — no child to her death. Curse? Simpler read: never repaired the relationship
    • Landing: P&A callback, two become one. Instead of growth or reunion → more division. David takes the crack and makes it wider
  • [S13–14 · 10:30] Stretched Past His Limit (~1.5 min) — not cruel, overextended: own time w/ God, kingdom, wives, kids. "You're on your own. Figure it out."
    • S14 In passing (10-30 sec): maybe uncomfortable w/ David's KIND of worship, or worship at all. Either way = David's opening to connect, help her grow
    • What if she'd been his only wife? (spitball: "What kind of worship ARE you comfortable with? Can we find it together?")
      • As David: "I remember when I first worshipped the Lord in the hills where I shepherded. Maybe I can show you and we can try worshipping together? Just the two of us?"
    • Landing: don't need 7 wives. Work, hobbies, ministry, thriving faith of your own, nothing left for theirs
  • [S15–16 · 12:00] The Accidental Legacy (~2 min)
    • Told Michal "figure it out," likely the whole family too. TOLD them to follow God, didn't MODEL it. What he did model taught something else
    • Wives, concubines, then Bathsheba: adultery, murder, lies (2 Sam 11). Lesson they took: want it? TAKE IT. Even if it hurts, even away from God, TAKE IT
    • Worse: Amnon/Tamar (13). Absalom murders, rebels (13–18). Solomon fears God but wealth/wives → kingdom splits (1 Kgs 11:11-13)
    • Landing: David's familial legacy. Not the one he meant, but what his kids picked up
  • [S17 · 14:00] Why Am I So Hard on David? (~1 min) — "Hard on David? Yes. Because I don't see David. I see myself." [Personal example]

Section 2 — Jehonadab's Legacy  (~15 min | 2 Kings 10, Jeremiah 35, Nehemiah 3)
  • Open: "David left a conflicted legacy. Let's revisit Jehonadab. What did he leave for his?"
  • A Quick Heads-Up (~15 sec) — Jehonadab / Jonadab, same guy. I'll mix them up
  • Jehu and Jehonadab (~1.25 min)
    • ~130 yrs after David. Kingdom split (1 Kgs 11:11-13). Northern Israel, idolatry, era of prophets
    • Seen only twice (2 Kgs 10:15, 23), speaks once. ~841 BC. Jehu wiping out Ahab's house (9:6-10), meets him on the road
    • Read (10:15): "Is your heart true to my heart as mine is to yours?" "It is." STOP reading there
    • Say it: Jehu pulls him up into his chariot (10:15)
  • Meanwhile, in Jerusalem (~3 min)
    • 200+ yrs later, ~598 BC. Move south to Judah. Babylon closing in on Jerusalem
    • slide: Over 200 Years Later Babylon dominant ~7 yrs. 1st siege (605) done, Daniel taken, another coming. ~330 yrs since split: 8 good kings, 10 bad. 15+ prophets by name (2 Chr 36:15-16). Jeremiah preaching ~28 yrs
    • God's odd command: wine in the temple, invite the Rechabites (35:1-2)
    • Who? Line of Jehonadab, son of Rechab (35:6). Learn more about him from descendants ~8 generations later
    • Picture it: countryside overrun by raiders. Big scary prophet invites you + all the grown men (35:3). Walking with dad, brothers, maybe you're the eldest leading. Table, bowls of wine, cups. "Come in, and drink some wine." (35:4-5)
  • "We Will Drink No Wine" (~3.25 min)
    • But no one steps forward. Why is the room suddenly tense?
    • Read the command (35:6-7): no wine, no house, no seed, no vineyard, tents all your days
    • Jehonadab vs. Jonadab = Jonathan vs. Jon (more in ref doc)
    • What mattered to him: no wine, houses, fields, vineyards. Tents. Closer to God, wanted family to experience God the same way
    • Read (35:8, 11): "We have obeyed..." ... "So we are living in Jerusalem."
    • SOME compromise already: tents → city walls, Babylon ransacking the countryside. Considered choice, desperate time
    • Most intimidating prophet alive. Devout men, know he speaks for God. Hospitality AND good-guest culture. Kingdom burning. Dare tell him no? Or "Eh, one more compromise?"
    • What would you do?
    • Their answer (35:6): "We will drink no wine..."
    • Landing: dead 200+ years, nobody in that room ever met him. Still won't touch it
  • God's Verdict, Acted Out (~5 min) — (35:12-19)
    • You just told Jeremiah no. Brace for impact... he gets up and leaves? What do you do? (popcorn, 5-10 sec)
    • Follow him out, right? (hide/sneak out → laugh) Curious. So were they
    • ~5 volunteers up front, no lines. Good sports
    • They're the Rechabites. Jeremiah at the front of the temple, facing Jerusalem
    • "Read 35:12-17 for what he actually said. This is the 'Paul translation.'"
    • Jeremiah to Judah (strained, elevated): FAITHLESS IDIOTS → prophets again, again, again → RETURN TO ME → YOU DO NOT LISTEN, DO NOT OBEY → are you dense? → I've been BEGGING → STIFF-NECKED, STUBBORN MORONS (35:13-15)
    • (gesture to volunteers) How are we feeling? (~10 sec)
    • (pull closest volunteer forward) JUDAH! See these guys? Forefather, 200+ YEARS AGO, said no wine. I said drink → THEY TOLD ME NO! ONE TIME! ONE GUY! They LISTENED AND OBEYED. Didn't need all the prophets. Not like YOUR THICK HEADS! (35:14, 16)
    • (let go, ~20 sec) NOW how are we feeling? (if they drift: "HOLD ON, RECHABITES, I'M NOT DONE WITH YOU YET")
    • Ends in judgment. Judah: "Yawn, doom every Tuesday"
    • Judgment: "God needs to try harder? Too hard to obey? YOU ARE WITHOUT EXCUSE! Babylon WILL come. City torn down, temple destroyed, carried off, scattered. God called, you did not answer!" (35:17; 25:8-11)
    • (turn, finger out) "AND AS FOR YOU!" (they jump) They were brave. Me? Squeak, hide behind the big guy
    • "AS FOR YOU, GOD BLESS YOU." Honored Jehonadab, kept his commands. Always a man in his house to serve God. GOD BLESS YOU. (35:18-19)
    • Thank + dismiss volunteers
  • Jerusalem Falls (~45 sec) — 586 BC (2 Kgs 25:1-21). Every bit as bad as warned. Read Lamentations
    • Landing: But what of the Rechabites?
  • They Persist! (~1.5 min)
    • Nehemiah callback, ~140 yrs after the fall, rebuilding walls. Read (Neh 3:14): "Malchijah the son of Rechab... repaired the Dung Gate."
    • Outlasted the fall of Jerusalem!
    • What about today? 1836, Joseph Wolff, 6 days near Sana'a, Yemen. "Sons of Jonadab." No wine, vineyards, sowing. Tents. Still quoting him (slide: Wolff portrait)
    • ~2,700 years after the chariot
    • Landing: trail grows cold. But I think someone's still out there in the house of Jehonadab, serving the Lord this way

Section 3 — Jesus's Legacy  (~16.5 min | Matthew 6, Luke 5, Matthew 26, Matthew 4)
  • Getting It to Stick (~1 min) — slide: Jesus's Legacy
    • David: great man, after God's own heart, but it didn't go beyond him. Kids picked up his worst habits
    • Jehonadab: no-name, handful of verses. Family followed him LITERALLY centuries. WHY?
    • Landing: father of two boys, I want to understand. Look to the One with the greatest legacy: Jesus
  • What I Said Last Time (~1.5 min) — slide: The Pushback
    • Taught before: he didn't just TELL, he SHOWED. Don't just tell kids how to pray, SHOW them
    • Pushback: Jesus said HIDE when you pray (Matt 6:5-6). Visible faith = bragging, even to our kids
    • Fascinating. I get things wrong, want feedback. Thought, prayed, read → we were BOTH right
  • What Jesus Actually Said (~1.5 min) — prayer focus (also giving, fasting)
    • Read (Matt 6:5-6): hypocrites... seen by others... "they have received their reward"... go into your room, shut the door
    • Pharisees: don't do that. But no curse. Goal = praise of men → got it. Congrats
    • Landing: what you set out to accomplish shapes HOW you do it
  • The Goal Determines the How (~2 min) — slide: grid
    • GoalHow?
      Praised by menPray loud, in public, draw attention
      Grow closer to GodPray privately and often
      Teach others to pray??? → Teach + model (Discipleship)
    • Pharisees got their reward. Praise of God / relationship → secret, and often (Luke 18:1). Teaching others = DIFFERENT goal → different HOW (animate ??? reveal)
    • FIRST time saying it: that's the answer to our question. Discipleship. Not just telling, not just doing it alone. Inviting others in to do it with you
    • Landing: same passage, right after "shut the door" → "Pray then like this," Lord's Prayer (6:9-13)
  • Growth Is Important (~1.5 min) — slide: Growth is Important
    • Private prayer still matters. Jesus DID go alone, often (Luke 5:16). Before dawn (Mark 1:35). All night before choosing the twelve (Luke 6:12)
    • Taught it too: "ought always to pray and not lose heart" (Luke 18:1)
    • Landing: discipleship doesn't replace your time with God, it depends on it. To help others grow, keep growing yourself
  • He Invited Them In (~3 min) — slide: Gethsemane
    • But also invited them in: Gethsemane. Peter, James, John in close, He goes a stone's throw farther
    • Why? "Watch with me." Always read it as wanting encouragement, and that's part of it. But "watch" = stay awake, stay alert, stay with me
    • Knew they wouldn't be much help: same night, "strike the shepherd, and the sheep will be scattered" (Matt 26:31). Wanted company, but knew He'd do this alone. Sheep near the shepherd as long as they can, for THEIR sake
    • What He DOESN'T do: never asks them to pray for Him. Doesn't send them off to private spots. If you pray, pray for YOURSELVES, to escape temptation in what's coming
    • Awake, close, not praying for Him → they're watching HIM. Not subtle: on His face, crying out, sweat like drops of blood. Read (Matt 26:39): "...let this cup pass from me; nevertheless, not as I will..."
    • Closed-door kind of prayer, but He invited them in. They kept falling asleep. Still, His actual words in 3 Gospels BECAUSE someone was close enough to hear
    • Same night, before the garden: prayed out loud over them, a whole chapter (John 17). Himself → them → "those who will believe through their word," all one as He and the Father are one (17:20-21). That's us. Prayed for His legacy, in front of the men He was discipling
    • Landing: take ANYTHING to God, emotions, all of it. Prayer mattered to Him. AND it's okay to share that kind of prayer with your followers
  • Let's Talk About Basketball (~2.5 min) — slide: Y'all Ball? → grid. Great player with kids. Revisit the prayer grid. Goal changes approach
    • Impress them: DUNK on them. No one gets better, but they know (you can still hoop)
    • Get better yourself: practice ON YOUR OWN. Before they wake, layups while they're at school, buddies after bedtime. Can't coach what you can't do
    • Teach them to love the game: invite them in, coach. Talk, practice, physically show. Show you LOVE it: "This is how YOU can love it too"
    • Landing: that's discipleship. How Jehonadab did it: found a playbook for his walk with God, invited his kids in. Not just rules: can't command tent-living from inside a house. Whole way of life, lived it WITH them
  • Discipleship: Not Limited to Just Family (~1.5 min) — slide: Discipleship
    • Jesus had no kids, still left the greatest legacy. Invited people: "Follow me" (Matt 4:19)
    • Jehonadab too? Some scholars: Rechabites more than blood. "Sons" = followers (2 Kgs 2:3). Family-based movement, others saw them love God in an idolatrous time and wanted in
    • Landing: no kids, or grown and gone? Still for you. Who could you invite in?
  • It's Not About the Tents (~45 sec) — slide: It's about what works. Not about no wine + tents
    • Landing: found what worked, brought family along. Lived it ~2,700 years
  • The Legacy I Want to Leave (~1.5 min)
    • I love the Bible. Read to them, AND prep lessons in front of them. Invites disruption, but loops them in

Close  (~6 min)
  • Back to David and Jehonadab (~1.5 min) — slide: Closing
    • David: great man, after God's own heart. TOLD his family, didn't walk alongside/disciple them. Failed to safeguard his kids. Didn't stick past him
    • Jehonadab did. Double dip: discipling safeguarded his family. Lives: outlasted Jerusalem's fall, helped rebuild. Faith: held on while Israel/Judah slid away, for centuries. AND God honored HIM by name, 200+ years later (Jer 35:18-19)
    • Landing: back to the grid. Pharisees: praise of men, got only that. Jehonadab: discipled his family, got praise of God too, through his kids
  • Our Family Poster (~1.5 min) — show poster
    • Lauren + me: trying to disciple our kids in what WE do to get closer to God and build His kingdom
    • "Humbly serve those in our influence, and boldly leverage everything for God's glory."
    • Everything comes down to that, so kids (and others) see how we love and serve God
    • Foster respite. 10 PM wheelchair fix. Struggling friend to dinner, cover the bill
    • Landing: not grandiose, but what my family is about
  • Yours Might Look Different (~1 min) — worshipping together, champion for the oppressed, modest self-denial like Jehonadab
  • Something to Look Back On (~1.5 min) — don't know if my kids will follow it
    • Landing: "If I want to connect with God, I can always try what worked for Dad."
