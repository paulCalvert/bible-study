Fear Not — God as Shield
Genesis 15:1. "After these things the word of the LORD came to Abram in a vision: 'Fear not, Abram, I am your shield; your reward shall be very great.'" The timing of God's appearance is not incidental — it follows immediately after a victory that put Abram in real danger. Notes on the context behind "fear not" and why God's first words to Abram here are a reassurance, not a commission.

Notes

"Fear not" was not generic encouragement — Abram had a real reason to be afraid Boice 527
"At this point, Abram was in great danger. Here were four powerful kings who had laid waste large areas of the land and carried off spoils from many cities. They had presumably spared Abram only because he was so insignificant and so far away. Now this nobody had attacked them. He had attacked with a small force, but he had won and recovered the spoils. They must have been furious, and Abram must have been wondering about the consequences of his heroic rescue of his nephew."

God's opening words in Genesis 15 — "Fear not" — land differently when you understand the situation. This is not God offering abstract comfort to a man who is vaguely anxious. This is God appearing to a man who has just provoked four regional powers and has every practical reason to expect retaliation. The shield language ("I am your shield") is military. God is addressing a specific, concrete fear with a specific, concrete promise.
