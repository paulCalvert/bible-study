Hagar and Ishmael
Genesis 16:1–16. Sarai proposes a solution to the heir problem. Abram agrees. Things go sour. Notes on how rationalization works when the suggestion comes from someone you trust, the pattern of blaming God when our own plans fail, and Abram's quiet act of faith in standing by Hagar's testimony.

Notes

Abram would have refused anyone else — but this was Sarai, and she had a point Boice 569
"No doubt if the suggestion had come from anyone else, Abram would have instantly refused. But this was Sarai, his wife. Moreover, she had a right to do this under the laws and customs of the time. Perhaps this was the way God intended for them to get an heir. Maybe Sarai had a point. Shouldn't he listen to his wife?"

The rationalization is layered and each layer is partially true: Sarai is his wife. The custom was legitimate. God had not ruled it out explicitly. Listening to your wife is generally good. None of those things are wrong on their own — which is what makes the reasoning so effective. The failure was not in any single argument but in substituting available logic for patient trust in God's timing.

Sarai blamed God — then blamed Abram — when her own plan failed Boice 570
Sarai says "The LORD has kept me from having children" — true, but with a belligerent note. She is framing her barrenness as something God did to her rather than a circumstance she is called to trust him through. Then when her plan with Hagar goes sour and Hagar begins to despise her, Sarai turns on Abram: "May the wrong done to me be on you!" (Gen 16:5).

Boice: "This always happens when we stop trusting God. We do wrong. We say God caused it to occur. Then, when our plans go sour, we blame either God or others for the outcome. The difficulty is not with God. The sin is in ourselves. The fault is in our own bad choices."

The sequence is predictable: distrust → self-made solution → failure → blame God or others. Sarai runs the whole pattern in a few verses.

Abram named the child Ishmael — based entirely on Hagar's testimony Boice 573
"Scripture never records that God told Abram the name. Yet verse 15 says, 'So Hagar bore Abram a son, and Abram gave the name Ishmael to the son she bore.' Apparently Hagar's testimony after she had returned to the camp convinced Abram that the God who revealed himself to him so many times before had now also appeared to her. So Abram stood by her in accepting the new revelation."

In the middle of a chapter full of failure, Boice highlights a quiet moment of grace: Abram believed Hagar. He received her account of the angel's words as genuine revelation and honored it by naming the child as instructed — even though he had not heard it himself. He extended the same faith to her encounter with God that he had exercised in his own.
