Abram Renamed Abraham
Genesis 17:1–8. God changes Abram's name to Abraham — "father of a multitude" — while he still has no son by Sarai. Notes on the social weight of that name, the camp dynamics that had built up around Ishmael, and the scene of Abraham announcing his new name to his tribe.

Notes

"Father of many" — the social weight of that name for a childless man Boice 576
Abram means "exalted father." Abraham means "father of a multitude." God was asking Abram to walk around every day with a name that declared a promise he could not yet see — to a man still without a son by his wife. In that culture, your name was not just an identifier; it was your identity and your story. Every time someone called him by name, every time he introduced himself, the name "father of a multitude" would hang in the air between him and a world that knew he had no heir. God was embedding the promise into his daily existence.

Camp dynamics — gossip, Abraham's pride in Ishmael, Sarah's humiliation Boice 577 · Barnhouse
By Genesis 17, Ishmael is 13 years old. Barnhouse and Boice paint a vivid portrait of what life in the camp must have looked like over those 13 years. Abraham had grown genuinely proud of Ishmael — the boy was his son, his heir by every human measure, and Abraham loved him. Hagar's status had risen with the birth. Sarah's humiliation had only deepened. The camp had reorganized itself around Ishmael as the future of the household. There would have been whispers, alliances, and a social reality that had hardened around the assumption that Ishmael was the promise. Into this settled dynamic, God arrives and upends everything.

Abraham announces his new name to the tribe — their incredulous reaction Boice 578–579 · Barnhouse
Boice and Barnhouse reconstruct the scene of Abraham gathering his household to announce the name change. Picture the moment: a 99-year-old man, with a 90-year-old wife who has never conceived, standing before his tribe and telling them God has renamed him "father of a multitude" — and that Sarai will bear a son within the year. The tribe would have exchanged glances. Some would have laughed under their breath. Some may have wondered if age was catching up with him. The social risk of this announcement was enormous. Abraham made it anyway — staking his credibility on a promise only God had given him.
