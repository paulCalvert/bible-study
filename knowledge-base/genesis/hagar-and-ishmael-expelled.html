Hagar and Ishmael Expelled
Genesis 21:8–21. Isaac is weaned. Ishmael mocks him. Sarah demands Hagar and Ishmael be sent away. Abraham is distressed — Ishmael is his son. But God confirms it: send them. Notes on the nature of God-required separations and how this painful moment was quietly preparing Abraham for what was coming in Genesis 22.

Notes

God sometimes requires separations — and the things he asks us to let go of seem fine Boice 663
"This raises some interesting personal questions. If you have served God for any length of time, you know that God has sometimes led you into situations like this. He requires separations, things you have to turn your back on — friends, occupations, pastimes, whatever it may be — and when you are in the midst of the struggle you look at these things and say, 'What's wrong with them? Aren't they all right?'"

Ishmael was not evil. Abraham loved him. The separation was not a punishment — it was a requirement for the next thing God was doing. Boice generalizes this into a pattern: God's required separations often involve things that are not obviously wrong. The difficulty is not that the thing is bad but that it is not the thing God has called you to. The struggle is real precisely because what you're letting go of has genuine value.

The separation was preparation — God was training Abraham for Genesis 22 Boice 664
"In view of what is going to come in Genesis 22 — when Abraham is going to be tested in his willingness to offer up his son Isaac — I tend to think of the separation as preparation of Abraham for the supreme test."

God did not ask Abraham to sacrifice Isaac without first asking him to release Ishmael. The lesser separation came first: let go of the son you love who is not the son of promise. Then, when the moment came to release the son who was the promise — Abraham had already learned, at cost, what it looked like to trust God with a son. The pattern of God's training is often visible only in hindsight. Each hard thing prepares for a harder thing, and the harder thing is where the deepest trust is formed.
