Isaac Born
Genesis 21:1–7. The promise kept. After decades of waiting, partial faith, doubt, and self-made solutions, God did exactly what he said he would do at exactly the time he said he would do it. Notes on what Isaac's birth teaches about God's faithfulness in spite of human unbelief, and on God's insistence on his own timing.

Notes

God was faithful in spite of human unbelief — the passage says it three times Boice 655
"One lesson they learned is that God is faithful to his promise. They had believed God partially, but they had doubted him too. At times they had tried to take matters into their own hands, thinking it was necessary for them to help God out. A year earlier, Sarah had utterly discounted the promise. But God was faithful in spite of human unbelief. This point is made clear by repetition. The passage says, 'Now the Lord was gracious to Sarah as he had said, and the Lord did for Sarah what he had promised. Sarah became pregnant and bore a son to Abraham in his old age, at the very time God had promised him.'"

Three phrases in two verses: "as he had said" — "what he had promised" — "at the very time God had promised." The repetition is not accidental. The text is making a point by hammering it: God said it. God did it. On time. Human doubt, human schemes, human failure — none of it moved the promise or delayed the delivery. The faithfulness was entirely on God's side.

God sent both Isaac and Jesus at his precise timing — not anyone else's Boice 658
Boice draws a parallel between Isaac's birth and the Incarnation. Both came at the exact moment God had determined — not early, not late, not when Abraham and Sarah were ready, not when the world expected it. Isaac came "at the very time God had promised" (Gen 21:2). Jesus came "when the fullness of time had come" (Gal 4:4). In both cases, God did not ask anyone's permission and did not adjust his schedule to human impatience or human despair. The timing itself was part of the miracle — chosen by God to make clear that what happened could only be explained by God.
