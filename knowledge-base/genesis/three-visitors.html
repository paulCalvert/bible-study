The Three Visitors
Genesis 18:1–8. Three visitors appear to Abraham at the oaks of Mamre. Notes on Abraham being called the friend of God — three times in Scripture — and on the identity of the visitors.

Notes

Abraham called "friend of God" — three times in Scripture Boice
Abraham is the only person in Scripture called "the friend of God" — and this title appears three times, across three different books: 2 Chronicles 20:7, Isaiah 41:8, and James 2:23. Each use carries weight on its own: in Chronicles it appears in the prayer of Jehoshaphat before battle; in Isaiah it is God himself using the term to describe Abraham; in James it is used to show that faith and works together are what genuine saving faith looks like — and Abraham is the proof. The repetition across the Old and New Testaments, and across such different contexts, suggests this was a settled and honored designation in the tradition of Israel.

Identity of the three visitors — likely Jesus and two angels, not the Trinity Boice 598–599
Boice argues the three visitors are most likely Jesus (a pre-incarnate Christophany) accompanied by two angels — rather than a manifestation of the Trinity. This reading is supported by the way the narrative unfolds: two of the visitors go on to Sodom (Gen 19:1 identifies them as "two angels"), while the LORD remains and speaks with Abraham. The one who stays and dialogues throughout is identified as the LORD (YHWH), which fits a Christophany better than a Trinitarian appearance. The Trinity does not appear corporally as three separate figures elsewhere in Scripture.
