Sarah's Laughter
Genesis 18:9–15. The visitors tell Abraham that Sarah will have a son within the year. Sarah, listening at the tent door, laughs. God hears it. But the name Isaac — "he laughs" — and Sarah's words at his birth (Gen 21:6) tell a different story about what laughter means in the end.

Notes

Sarah's laughter at the promise and her laughter at the birth were not the same laugh Gen 18:9–15 · Gen 21:6
In Genesis 18, Sarah laughs when she overhears the promise — the laugh of a 90-year-old woman who has waited decades and no longer believes it is possible. God calls her out on it. She denies it. It is the laughter of unbelief, and she knows it.

In Genesis 21:6, when Isaac is born, Sarah says: "God has brought me laughter, and everyone who hears about this will laugh with me." Isaac's name means "he laughs." The same sound that came from unbelief at the tent door became the name of the miracle, and finally the expression of unbridled joy and wonder at what God had done.

God did not change the name. He redeemed the laughter. What began as doubt ended as worship — and the joy was deeper because of how long and how impossible the wait had been.
