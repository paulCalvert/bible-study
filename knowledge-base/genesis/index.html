Genesis
Theological notes, commentary citations, and biblical background drawn from the book of Genesis — organized by chapter for reference across all present and future studies.

Genesis 13

Lot — Character Arc Gen 13–19
Lot started near Sodom thinking he would not be pulled in — and ended up living there fully. Background and contrast material: his wealth possessing him, the slippery slope of proximity to sin, and his position as city elder with apparently zero converts.
Gen 13–19 Character Study Contrast Material Temptation Boice

Genesis 14

Abraham Rescues Lot Gen 14:1–16
Abram was peaceful by nature but had trained 318 fighting men — he was prepared even when he didn't expect to need it. Notes on his motivation (Lot as family and fellow believer, not a fool deserving judgment) and the tactical detail of the counterattack.
Gen 14:1–16 Narrative Character Study Preparedness Boice
Melchizedek Gen 14:18–20 · Heb 5–7 · Ps 110
King of Salem and priest of God Most High — a figure who appears briefly in Genesis and becomes a major theological lens for Christ's priesthood in Hebrews. Notes on his significance, the Levitical contrast, F.B. Meyer on the blended roles, and Arthur Pink on Hebrews 10:24–25.
Gen 14 Heb 5–7 Ps 110 Typology Priesthood Boice · F.B. Meyer · Arthur Pink
Abram Declines the Spoils Gen 14:21–24
After rescuing Lot and meeting Melchizedek, Abram refuses everything the King of Sodom offers. Notes on the world's assumptions about men like Abram, the sequence of Melchizedek before Sodom, and why Abram didn't require his allies to follow his standard.
Gen 14:21–24 Wealth & Worldliness Discipleship Contentment Boice

Genesis 15

Fear Not — God as Shield Gen 15:1
God's first words to Abram in Genesis 15 are "fear not" — and the timing is not incidental. Abram had just defeated four powerful kings with a small force. They had every reason to retaliate. God's reassurance is military and specific, not generic.
Gen 15:1 Fear & Faith God's Protection Boice
Abram Questions God About an Heir Gen 15:2–5
God promises a great reward — Abram asks what good it is without a son. Notes on the difference between honest complaint brought directly to God and the murmuring that never reaches him at all. God answered the honest question with the stars.
Gen 15:2–5 Prayer & Honesty Complaint & Lament Boice
Abram Believed God Gen 15:6
The verse Paul quotes in Romans 4 and Galatians 3. No one is good by God's standard — the captain story (Barnhouse) and the Spurgeon conversion story both illustrate what it means to simply look to God rather than to yourself. Faith counted as righteousness.
Gen 15:6 Rom 4 Faith & Justification Illustration Boice · Barnhouse · Spurgeon
The Covenant Ceremony Gen 15:7–21
God ratifies his promise through an ancient covenant-cutting ceremony — and passes through the pieces alone. Notes on "I have given" (present tense, already settled), the firepot and torch symbolism (1 Pet 1:7, Mal 3:3, John 1:5), Abram's question vs. Zechariah's doubt, and why God walking alone means the promise rests entirely on him.
Gen 15:7–21 1 Pet 1:7 John 1:5 Covenant Theology Grace & Unilateral Promise Symbolism Boice

Genesis 16

Hagar and Ishmael Gen 16:1–16
Abram would have refused anyone else — but this was Sarai, and each layer of her reasoning was partially true. Notes on how rationalization works, the predictable pattern of blaming God when self-made plans fail, and Abram's quiet faith in accepting Hagar's testimony as genuine revelation.
Gen 16:1–16 Doubt & Self-Reliance Rationalization Blame & Deflection Boice

Genesis 17

Abram Renamed Abraham Gen 17:1–8
The social weight of the name "father of a multitude" for a childless 99-year-old. The camp dynamics around Ishmael after 13 years. The scene of Abraham announcing his new name to his tribe — and their incredulous reaction.
Gen 17:1–8 Identity & Promise Faith in the Unseen Boice · Barnhouse
Names, Breath, and Circumcision Gen 17:9–22
The "h" breath added to Abram's name. God's name (YHWH) as one enormous breath. El-Shaddai — first use in Scripture, rooted in the word for female breast. Circumcision as the cutting away of human effort, not a contribution to the covenant.
Gen 17:9–22 Names of God Covenant Signs Word Study Boice
Circumcision of the Household Gen 17:23–27
Children included in the covenant sign — parallel to the baptism question. Ishmael circumcised despite not being the child of promise. NT silence on infant baptism as evidence of broad early agreement.
Gen 17:23–27 Col 2:11–12 Covenant Theology Household Faith Baptism Parallel Boice

Genesis 18

Abraham Intercedes for Sodom Gen 18:16–33
God is not ignorant of wickedness and not indifferent to the oppressed — but we have grown numb to cries he still hears. Abraham's intercession works because he doesn't ask God to make an exception; he appeals to God's own nature and holds him to his own character.
Gen 18:16–33 Prayer & Intercession God's Justice Compassion & Numbness Boice
Sarah's Laughter Gen 18:9–15
Sarah laughed at the promise in unbelief. God called her out. Then Isaac was born — and his name means "he laughs." The same sound that came from doubt became the name of the miracle and finally the expression of unbridled joy at what God had done.
Gen 18:9–15 Gen 21:6 Faith & Doubt God Redeems
The Three Visitors Gen 18:1–8
Abraham called "friend of God" — three times across 2 Chronicles, Isaiah, and James. The identity of the three visitors: likely a pre-incarnate Christ and two angels rather than the Trinity. Note: Boice 598–599 argument incomplete — pending additional notes.
Gen 18:1–8 Friendship with God Christophany Boice

Genesis 20

Abraham Deceives Abimelech Gen 20
The same failure as Egypt — after the circumcision, after the three visitors, after the promise of Isaac within the year. The sin was cowardly and dishonoring to God. Abraham slandered Abimelech by assuming no fear of God in him — when Abimelech turned out to be the one with a genuine conscience.
Gen 20 Failure & Sin Slander & Assumption Moral Irony Boice

Genesis 21

Hagar and Ishmael Expelled Gen 21:8–21
God requires separations from things that aren't obviously wrong — the struggle is real because what you're letting go of has genuine value. And this separation was preparation: letting go of the son he loved who wasn't the son of promise trained Abraham for the moment he'd have to release the son who was.
Gen 21:8–21 Gen 22 Separation & Letting Go God's Training Boice
Isaac Born Gen 21:1–7
God was faithful in spite of partial faith, doubt, and self-made solutions — the text says it three times in two verses. And God sent both Isaac and Jesus at his own precise timing, not anyone else's. The timing itself was part of the miracle.
Gen 21:1–7 Gal 4:4 God's Faithfulness Divine Timing Typology Boice
