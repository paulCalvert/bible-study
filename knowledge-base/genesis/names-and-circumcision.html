Names, Breath, and Circumcision
Genesis 17:9–22. The theological weight embedded in the name change and the sign of circumcision. Notes on the "h" breath added to Abram's name, God's own name as breath, El-Shaddai's first appearance in Scripture, and what circumcision signifies about human effort and the covenant.

The Name — Breath and Identity

The "h" added to Abram's name is the sound of a breath Boice 583
When God changes Abram to Abraham, the addition is the Hebrew letter *heh* — which is simply the sound of an exhaled breath. Boice notes this is theologically significant: God is, in a sense, breathing himself into Abraham's name. The name change is not merely a rebranding — it is an impartation. God's breath, which animated Adam in Genesis 2:7, is now woven into the name Abraham carries.
Gen 17:5 Gen 2:7 Names & Identity Word Study Boice
God's name — Jehovah — is spelled with four vowels; it is one enormous breath Boice 584
The Hebrew name for God — YHWH (Yahweh / Jehovah) — is composed entirely of vowels. Boice notes that it cannot be spoken without breathing; to say the name of God is to breathe. Some have observed that the name itself sounds like breath — Yah (inhale) Weh (exhale). The God who breathed life into Adam, who breathed his letter into Abraham's name, has a name that is itself breath. Life, speech, and the presence of God are intertwined at the level of language.

El-Shaddai — God Almighty

El-Shaddai — first use of "God Almighty" in Scripture; shad means female breast Boice 584–585
Genesis 17:1 is the first time God reveals himself as El-Shaddai — translated "God Almighty." Boice notes that the root word *shad* in Hebrew means female breast. El-Shaddai therefore carries the sense of "the all-sufficient one" — the God who nourishes, sustains, and provides everything needed. It is not a name of raw power (El alone covers that) but of sufficiency and provision. The name is introduced specifically in the context of an impossible pregnancy — God presenting himself to a 99-year-old man as the one who is enough for what is about to happen.

Circumcision — The Sign of the Covenant

Circumcision = cutting away of flesh = renunciation of human effort Boice 585
Circumcision was not a contribution to the covenant — it was a sign of it. Boice emphasizes that the cutting away of flesh symbolizes the renunciation of human effort and self-sufficiency. The very organ that represented Abraham's ability to produce an heir was marked with a sign that said: this is not going to happen by your power. The covenant rests on God, not on human capacity. Circumcision was a daily, embodied reminder that the promise came from outside Abraham — not from within him.
