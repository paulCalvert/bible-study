Abram Believed God
Genesis 15:6. "And he believed the LORD, and he counted it to him as righteousness." One of the most theologically loaded verses in the Old Testament — picked up by Paul in Romans 4 and Galatians 3. Notes on what it means that no one is good enough apart from God, and two illustrations of what it looks like to simply look to God for salvation.

Notes

The captain story — by God's standard, no one is good Boice 553–554 · Donald Grey Barnhouse
A rich man buys a boat, staffs it with sailors, and brings his mother aboard. He dresses in full captain's uniform and strikes a pose: "Look, Momma, I'm a captain!" His mother surveys him calmly and replies: "Sonny, by you, you is a captain, and by me, you is a captain — but by captains, you is no captain."

Barnhouse applied it directly to our standing before God: "By you, you're good; by me, you're good — but by God, you're no good. In his sight you have no goodness at all."

The point is not that people are incapable of moral behavior — it is that our standard and God's standard are not the same scale. Abram's faith was counted as righteousness not because Abram was impressive by human measure but because God's measure is different, and only faith bridges that gap.

The Spurgeon conversion story — "Look! Look! Look! You have nothing to do but look and live." Boice 559 · Isaiah 45:22
Spurgeon as a young man wandered into a Primitive Methodist chapel on a winter morning. The minister hadn't come — a layman was filling in. The text was Isaiah 45:22: "Look unto me, and be ye saved, all the ends of the earth."

The layman preached simply: "My dear friends, this is a very simple text indeed. It just says 'Look.' Now lookin' don't take a great deal of pain. It ain't liftin' your foot or your finger; it is just 'Look.' Well, a man needn't go to college to learn to look. You may be the biggest fool, and yet you can look. A man needn't be worth a thousand pounds a year to be able to look. Anyone can look; even a child can look. But then the text says, 'Look unto ME.' Ay! Many of you are lookin' to yourselves, but it's no use lookin' there. You'll never find any comfort in yourselves. Look to Christ. The text says, 'Look unto ME.'"

He noticed Spurgeon in the congregation — young, visibly struggling — and addressed him directly: "Young man, you look miserable, and you will always be miserable — miserable in life, and miserable in death — if you don't obey my text... Young man, look to Jesus Christ. Look! Look! Look! You have nothing to do but to look and live."

Spurgeon looked. That was his conversion. Boice connects it to Genesis 15:6 because Abram's faith worked the same way — not effort, not achievement, not earning. Just looking to God. The righteousness came from the direction of the gaze, not the quality of the man.
